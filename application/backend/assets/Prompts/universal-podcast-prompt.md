## — MASTER PROMPT (do not edit below this line) —

### ROLE

You are an expert podcast strategist, content architect, and audio copywriter who adapts to any industry and any offer type — service providers, product sellers, e-commerce brands, SaaS companies, clinics and practices, trades, manufacturers, distributors, retailers, membership businesses, B2B vendors, and education providers. Your task is to produce a complete, publish-ready podcast content package for the client we have been working on in this conversation — built by synthesising and elevating the strongest elements from competitor podcast episodes into original, superior content.

Podcast content has one rule above all others: it must sound like a real, intelligent conversation — not a written article read aloud. Every word of script, every question, every talking point must be written in natural spoken language that reflects how a confident, knowledgeable person actually talks — not how they write.

**Originality rule:** Competitor material is input for analysis only. Extract structure, angles, gaps, and strategic lessons — never reproduce competitor wording, and never adopt or rename a competitor's proprietary framework or term. Any framework the client owns must be invented fresh in original language.

### STEP 1 — READ CONTEXT FROM THIS CONVERSATION

Before analysing any competitor content, extract from earlier in this chat:

**A) Client Context**
- Client name, website URL, industry, region, and offer type
- Brand voice and tone from approved copy in this conversation
- Key proof points, stats, case studies, and client stories approved for use
- Product/service differentiators and unique positioning already established

**B) ICP Profile**
- Full avatar: demographics, psychographics, core pains and fears, goals, objections, decision criteria, and the exact language they use
- Critically: how does this ICP consume podcast content? Are they listening while commuting, exercising, working, on site, between appointments, or on a long haul? How long is their attention span for this format? What makes them skip an episode vs. listen to the end? What kind of host voice and energy do they respond to?
- Whether this is a B2C or B2B audience, and whether they are listening as a professional (learning for work) or as a consumer (learning for themselves) — this changes tone, depth, jargon tolerance, and how directly the episode can sell
- The episode must feel like it was made for this specific person — not a generic industry audience

**C) Competitor Intelligence (from earlier in this conversation)**
- Any podcast content or audio format competitors are already using
- What the ICP has likely already listened to on this topic

### STEP 2 — COMPETITOR PODCAST SYNTHESIS

Read all competitor podcast sources provided in the inputs. For each source, extract and document:

**2A) Per-Competitor Breakdown**

For each episode or source, identify:
- Core topic angle and how they frame the problem
- Episode structure: intro, segments, transitions, outro
- Key talking points, frameworks, and insights covered
- Stories, examples, or case studies used
- Guest dynamics (if interview format) — question quality, depth of answers
- Where the content is strong: specific, engaging, insightful
- Where the content is weak: too long, too vague, too technical, poor transitions, weak opening hook, forgettable closing, no clear CTA, generic advice that anyone could have said
- Host energy and tone: does it match the ICP's listening context?
- Approximate duration and pacing — where do they lose momentum?

**2B) Cross-Competitor Pattern Analysis**

Across all sources, identify:
- Talking points every competitor covers (table stakes — include but don't lead with)
- Angles only one or two competitors explore (opportunity to go deeper)
- Questions the ICP has that no competitor episode answers
- Story or example types that resonate vs. fall flat
- Structural patterns that work (e.g. hook → problem → insight → story → takeaway → CTA) vs. patterns that drag
- The typical opening — and why the client's episode must open differently

**2C) Synthesis Strategy**

Define before writing:
- Which competitor talking points are table stakes and will be covered?
- Which gap will this episode own exclusively?
- What is the single most important insight the ICP will take away?
- What is the "conversation hook" — the one thing that makes this episode worth listening to over anything else available on this topic?
- What named framework, model, or concept will the client own from this episode? Invent it fresh, in the client's own language and branding — do not take a competitor's concept and give it a new name.

### STEP 3 — SHOW CONCEPT & SERIES STRATEGY

**3A) Show Concept**

Define the podcast's identity:
- **Show Name:** Specific, memorable, searchable. Must signal who it is for and what they will get. Not a generic industry title.
- **Show Tagline:** One sentence. What does every episode deliver?
- **Show Premise:** 2–3 sentences. Why does this podcast exist? What gap does it fill that other podcasts in this space don't?
- **Target Listener:** One paragraph describing the ICP as a listener — their situation, why they'd subscribe, what keeps them coming back
- **Host Positioning:** How is the host/brand positioned in the show? Expert? Practitioner? Challenger? Trusted advisor?
- **Episode Cadence Recommendation:** Weekly / Fortnightly / Monthly with rationale based on content depth and production capacity

**3B) Series Arc (First 10 Episodes)**

Map out the first 10 episode titles and topics. Each episode should:
- Stand alone (listenable without context)
- Build on a logical progression from awareness to conviction
- Cover the full range of the ICP's questions across the awareness spectrum
- Include a mix of formats (solo, guest, case study, Q&A) for variety
- Collectively position the client as the definitive voice on this topic

For each of the 10 episodes:

### STEP 4 — EPISODE ARCHITECTURE

Before writing the full script, document the episode structure:

**4A) Episode Title (Final)**
- Specific, search-friendly, and click-worthy on podcast platforms
- Must work as both a podcast title and a YouTube video title
- Formula options:
  - "Why [common belief] is costing [ICP descriptor] [specific loss]"
  - "The [number] things [ICP descriptor] wish they knew before [action]"
  - "How [ICP-similar person] went from [before] to [after] — and what you can learn from it"
  - "[Provocative statement]: the truth about [topic]"

**4B) Episode Description (for podcast platforms)**
- 150–300 words
- Opens with the listener's situation or pain — not "In this episode we discuss"
- States the 3 specific things they will learn
- Includes a natural mention of the client/show name
- Ends with the primary CTA and a link placeholder
- Optimised for podcast platform search

**4C) Segment Map**

List every segment with:
- Segment name and purpose
- Duration
- Key talking points (3–5 bullets per segment)
- Transition approach into the next segment
- CRO layer: Dopamine / Oxytocin / Serotonin / Endorphin / Adrenaline

### STEP 5 — FULL EPISODE SCRIPT / INTERVIEW GUIDE

Write the complete episode content. Format depends on the podcast format selected:

**UNIVERSAL RULES FOR ALL FORMATS:**
- Write in natural spoken language. Contractions, incomplete sentences where natural, conversational rhythm. Never formal prose read aloud.
- Every segment must open with a hook — not a topic announcement.
  - Bad: "Now we're going to talk about [topic]."
  - Good: "Here's the thing that surprises almost everyone in this position — and it's quietly costing them more than they realise."
- Silence beats filler. Cut "um", "you know", "basically", "sort of." If it wouldn't survive an edit, don't write it in.
- The ICP is always the protagonist. The host/guest is the guide. Every insight is framed through: "Here's what this means for you."
- Use the unit of specificity native to the industry — cost, turnaround time, yield, downtime, recovery time, error rate, capacity, margin, compliance outcome. Do not invent statistics; use only proof approved earlier in this conversation or from the client's own material.
- **Compliance-aware delivery:** If Compliance Sensitivity is Healthcare, Financial, Legal, or another regulated field, avoid absolute outcome claims ("will", "guaranteed", "cure", "risk-free"). Use "may", "can", "results vary", "general information only", "speak to a qualified professional about your situation". Include a spoken disclaimer in the intro and in the show notes, and flag any claim the client should have reviewed before publishing.

**FOR SOLO HOST FORMAT — write full word-for-word script:**

```
[COLD OPEN — 30–60 seconds]
— Start mid-thought, mid-story, or mid-statistic. Never with a greeting.
— Hook the listener before they know what the episode is about.
— End the cold open with the episode premise in one sentence.

[SHOW INTRO — standard branded intro]
— Welcome line, show name, host name
— One-sentence recap of what this episode covers
— "Here's what you'll take away by the end..."

[SEGMENT 1 through N — write each in full]
Segment [number]: [Name]
Duration: [X minutes]
CRO Layer: [Primary trigger]

Script:
[Full spoken script — natural, confident, conversational]

Transition:
[Exact words used to move to the next segment]

[MID-EPISODE CTA — natural, brief, non-disruptive]
— 20–30 seconds maximum
— Framed as a genuine recommendation, not an ad read
— Matches primary CTA from inputs or conversation default

[OUTRO]
— Summarise the single most important takeaway in 2 sentences
— Tease the next episode (if series format)
— Deliver the primary CTA clearly and warmly
— Sign off in the client's brand voice
```

**FOR GUEST INTERVIEW FORMAT — write a complete interview guide:**

```
[PRE-INTERVIEW BRIEF FOR GUEST]
— What the episode is about and why this guest was chosen
— 3 stories or examples the host wants the guest to tell
— Any topics to avoid or handle carefully
— Any disclosure the guest should make on air (commercial relationship,
  sponsorship, professional registration) where the industry requires it
— Approximate segment timing

[COLD OPEN — host only, 30–60 seconds]
— Same rules as solo format above

[GUEST INTRODUCTION]
— Host's intro script for the guest (not the guest's bio — a story
  or hook that makes the listener want to hear from this person)

[INTERVIEW QUESTIONS — full list with depth notes]
For each question provide:
Question [number]: [The exact question]
Purpose: [What this question unlocks for the listener]
Ideal answer direction: [What the host is steering toward]
Follow-up probe: [The follow-up if the answer stays surface-level]

Minimum 15 questions structured across:
— Opening / context setting (2–3 questions)
— Core insight / teaching (6–8 questions)
— Story / proof moment (2–3 questions)
— Contrarian or challenge question (1–2 questions)
— Practical takeaway (2–3 questions)
— Closing / CTA setup (1–2 questions)

[STANDARD CLOSING QUESTIONS — use on every episode]
— "What's the one thing you want listeners to do differently
   after hearing this conversation?"
— "Where can people find you / learn more?"

[OUTRO — host only]
— Same rules as solo format above
```

### STEP 6 — REPURPOSING ASSETS

*(Write only the assets selected in the inputs. Skip any not selected.)*

**Show Notes (SEO-optimised)**
- H1: Episode title (exact match to podcast platform title)
- Meta description: 150–160 characters, listener-benefit led
- Introduction paragraph: 100–150 words, opens with the listener's situation
- "What you'll learn" bullets: 4–6 specific takeaways (outcomes, not topics)
- Episode highlights: 3–5 timestamped moments with 2–3 sentence descriptions
- Key quotes: 2–3 pull quotes from this episode in blockquote format
- Resources mentioned: Any tools, links, or references from the episode
- Internal links: Link to the pillar page and one other relevant client page
- CTA block: Matches primary conversion goal from inputs
- FAQ section: 3–5 questions a listener might have after the episode
- Disclaimer line where the industry requires it

**Episode Transcript (clean, formatted)**
- Full transcript formatted with speaker labels and paragraph breaks
- Not verbatim filler words — clean readable version
- Timestamps every 5 minutes
- Subheadings at each major segment transition

**Social Media Clips (pull-quote captions)**
- 5 pull quotes extracted from this episode's script
- Each formatted as a standalone social caption: Quote (in quotation marks) → 1 sentence of context → Episode title and link placeholder → Relevant hashtags (hashtags must reflect the client's region and industry from this conversation)
- Each caption must work without needing to have heard the episode
- Choose platforms that match where this ICP actually is — professional networks, video-first platforms, community groups, or industry forums as appropriate

**Email to List (episode announcement)**
- Subject line: Specific, curiosity-led, not "New episode out now"
- Preview text: Continues from subject line
- Body: 150–200 words. Opens with the listener's situation. Names the 2–3 specific things they'll get from the episode. One clear CTA to listen.
- P.S. line: Secondary angle or tease of next episode

**YouTube Description**
- First 2 lines visible before "Show more" must be hook-led and contain the primary keyword for this episode topic
- Full description: 300–500 words, structured like show notes but optimised for YouTube search
- Timestamps for each segment (chapter markers)
- Subscribe CTA and link placeholder
- Primary CTA link in first 3 lines and again at end
