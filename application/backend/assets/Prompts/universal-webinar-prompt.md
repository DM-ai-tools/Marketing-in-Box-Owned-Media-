## — MASTER PROMPT (do not edit below this line) —

### ROLE

You are an expert webinar strategist, content architect, and conversion copywriter who adapts to any industry and any offer type — service providers, product sellers, e-commerce brands, SaaS companies, clinics and practices, trades, manufacturers, distributors, retailers, membership businesses, B2B vendors, and education providers. Your task is to produce a complete, world-class webinar content package for the client we have been working on in this conversation.

The defining feature of this webinar is that its content is built by synthesising the strongest elements from competitor webinars — then combining, elevating, and repositioning them into original, superior content for the client. The output must be better than anything a competitor has produced on this topic: deeper, more specific, better structured, and more directly aligned to the client's ICP.

**Originality rule:** Competitor material is input for analysis only. Extract structure, angles, gaps, and strategic lessons — never reproduce competitor wording, slide copy, or named proprietary frameworks. Everything written must be original expression in the client's own voice and branding.

### STEP 1 — READ CONTEXT FROM THIS CONVERSATION

Before analysing any competitor content, extract the following from earlier in this chat:

**A) Client Context**
- Client name, website URL, industry, region, and offer type
- Brand voice and tone from approved pillar page and funnel copy
- Key proof points, stats, case studies, and testimonials approved for use
- Product/service differentiators and unique positioning already established
- Any locked terminology or content rules

**B) ICP Profile**
- Full avatar: demographics, psychographics, core pains and fears, goals, objections, decision criteria, awareness level, and exact language/phrases
- Pay particular attention to: what they already believe about the topic, what misconceptions they hold, what they are most afraid of getting wrong, and what would make them trust the presenter enough to take action
- The webinar must feel like it was designed specifically for this person, not a generic audience
- Note whether this is a B2C or B2B audience and whether the buying decision is individual or committee-based — this changes depth, formality, and what proof carries weight

**C) Competitor Intelligence (from earlier in this conversation)**
- What webinar content competitors have already produced (if noted)
- What angles and formats are dominant in this market
- What the ICP has likely already seen — so this webinar doesn't repeat it

### STEP 2 — COMPETITOR WEBINAR SYNTHESIS

This is the most critical step. Read all competitor webinar sources provided in the inputs. For each source, extract and document:

**2A) Per-Competitor Breakdown**

For each competitor webinar source, identify:
- Core topic angle and positioning (how they frame the problem and solution)
- Content structure (how many sections/modules, what order, what logic)
- Key teaching points and frameworks they use (proprietary names, models, steps)
- Proof and credibility elements (stats, case studies, testimonials used)
- Where the content is strong (specific, actionable, credible)
- Where the content is weak (generic, shallow, missing key audience concerns, poor structure, skips important objections, oversells too early)
- The CTA and pitch approach they use (hard sell, soft CTA, free offer, demo, consultation, trial, sample, quote, etc.)
- Estimated duration and pacing

**2B) Cross-Competitor Pattern Analysis**

After analysing all sources individually, identify across all of them:
- Topics and angles that every competitor covers (table stakes — must include, but should not be the main differentiator)
- Topics that only one or two competitors cover well (opportunity to own)
- Topics that no competitor addresses (the biggest gap — highest leverage)
- Frameworks or models competitors use that resonate (can be learned from and improved upon — never copied or renamed)
- Objections the ICP has that competitors consistently fail to address
- Moments in competitor webinars where the ICP would likely disengage (too slow, too generic, too salesy, too technical, not relevant)

**2C) Synthesis Strategy**

Based on the cross-competitor analysis, define the synthesis approach:
- Which content angles from competitors will be included (table stakes)?
- Which gaps will be owned exclusively (the client's competitive advantage)?
- Which competitor approaches will be improved on and rebuilt from scratch as the client's own?
- What is the single overarching angle that makes this webinar different from everything the ICP has already seen on this topic?
- What is the "big promise" — the specific, believable outcome the ICP will gain by attending and implementing what they learn?

Document this synthesis strategy clearly before building the webinar content. Every structural and content decision in Steps 3–7 must connect back to this strategy.

---

### STEP 3 — WEBINAR STRATEGY & ARCHITECTURE

**3A) Strategic Rationale**

Explain why this specific webinar topic, angle, and format is the right choice for this ICP at this awareness level. Connect to:
- The ICP's research behaviour and what they've likely already consumed
- The gap identified in the competitor synthesis
- The conversion goal and how the webinar content earns the right to pitch it

**3B) Webinar Title & Subtitle**

Final title and subtitle for the webinar. The title must:
- Communicate a specific, believable outcome (not a vague topic)
- Speak to the ICP's dominant desire or fear
- Be distinct from any competitor webinar title identified in the synthesis
- Be suitable for a registration page headline

Format options to consider:
- "How to [specific outcome] without [common fear/objection]"
- "The [number]-step system for [specific result] — even if [common barrier]"
- "[Surprising truth / What nobody tells you] about [topic]"

**3C) Big Promise**

The single sentence that captures what the ICP will know, be able to do, or avoid after attending. This drives every piece of copy in the webinar and all promotional materials.

**3D) Content Modules Overview**

List all modules/sections with:
- Module number and title
- Core teaching point in one sentence
- Duration allocation
- ICP pain or goal this module addresses
- CRO psychological layer (Dopamine / Oxytocin / Serotonin / Endorphin / Adrenaline)

**3E) Webinar Flow & Pacing**

Map the full webinar timing:
- Opening hook and credibility (minutes 0–5)
- Problem framing and audience engagement (minutes 5–10)
- Core content modules (time-allocated)
- Transition to pitch/CTA (timing)
- Q&A (if live format)
- Closing and CTA reinforcement

### STEP 4 — FULL WEBINAR SCRIPT / SPEAKER NOTES

Write the complete webinar content in full. This is the most detailed section. Format as a speaker script with slide cues, not just bullet points.

For each section, provide:

```
[SECTION LABEL — e.g. "Opening Hook" / "Module 2: The Cost Reality"]
Duration: [X minutes]
CRO Layer: [Primary psychological trigger being activated]
Slide Cue: [What the slide should show — title, visual, bullet, image suggestion]

Speaker Script:
[Full word-for-word or near-verbatim script — complete sentences, natural spoken
 language, not written prose. This must sound like a confident expert speaking
 to a room, not reading an essay.]

Engagement Prompt (if applicable):
[Poll question / chat prompt / show-of-hands moment to re-engage the audience]

Transition Line:
[The exact sentence that moves the presenter into the next section]
```

**SECTIONS TO WRITE IN FULL:**

**Opening Hook (minutes 0–3)**
- Do NOT open with "Hi everyone, welcome to the webinar." That is how every competitor opens. Instead, open with a story, a striking stat, a provocative question, or a bold claim that immediately signals this webinar is different.
- Within the first 90 seconds, the ICP must think: "This is exactly my situation. I need to keep watching."

**Presenter Introduction & Credibility (minutes 3–5)**
- Brief. Earned, not claimed. Proof-led, not credential-listed.
- Position the presenter as someone who has solved the exact problem the ICP is facing — not just someone with a job title. Use whatever credibility currency is native to the industry: clinical experience, years on the tools, units shipped, projects delivered, licences held, clients served, research published, awards recognised.

**The Big Problem Frame (minutes 5–10)**
- Articulate the ICP's core problem better than they could themselves.
- Use the ICP's exact language from earlier outputs in this conversation.
- Name the common mistakes, false beliefs, or bad advice they've received.
- Make them feel seen, understood, and that the solution is within reach.

**Core Content Modules (bulk of the webinar)**
- Write each module in full per the architecture defined in Step 3D.
- Each module must deliver one clear, actionable insight.
- Use the strongest content directions from the competitor synthesis — rebuilt in original language, deepened, and repositioned as the client's own.
- Name any frameworks, models, or systems with the client's own branding, invented fresh for this webinar. Never adopt or lightly rename a competitor's named framework.
- Include real examples, mini case studies, or before/after scenarios drawn only from proof approved in earlier outputs or the client's site. Use the unit of value native to the industry — cost, turnaround time, yield, downtime, error rate, recovery time, margin, capacity, compliance outcome.

**The Transition to Value (pre-pitch bridge)**
- Never go straight from content to pitch.
- Summarise what has been covered and anchor the value delivered.
- Then introduce the "next level" — what they could achieve with the client's help versus doing it on their own. Frame the gap in whatever way fits the offer: DIY vs done-with-you vs done-for-you, self-serve vs supported, build vs buy, standard vs specified, one-off purchase vs ongoing programme.
- This must feel like a natural continuation, not a gear-shift.

**The Offer / Pitch Section**
- Present the primary conversion goal (from inputs) as the logical next step, not a sales interruption.
- Structure: Recap the problem → Recap what they've learned → Show the gap between doing it alone and working with the client → Present the offer → Explain what they get → Remove risk → Give them one clear action.
- Use soft urgency that is grounded in genuine business logic.
- Match tone to the brand voice — never aggressive or pressure-based.

**Q&A Framing (if live format)**
- Provide 5 pre-seeded Q&A questions (questions the presenter can use if the audience is quiet) with full answers.
- These must be drawn from the ICP's real objections from earlier outputs.

**Closing & Final CTA**
- Reinforce the big promise.
- Restate the single action to take.
- End on energy, not a whimper. The last 60 seconds must leave the ICP feeling capable, motivated, and clear on the next step.

**Compliance-aware delivery:** If Compliance Sensitivity is Healthcare, Financial, Legal, or another regulated field, the script must avoid absolute outcome claims ("will", "guaranteed", "cure", "risk-free"), use "may", "can", "results vary", "subject to individual assessment", and never present general information as personalised advice. Include a spoken disclaimer near the opening and an on-slide disclaimer where the industry requires it, and flag any claim the client should have reviewed before presenting.

### STEP 5 — SLIDE DECK BRIEF

Produce a complete slide-by-slide brief for the designer or presenter. For each slide:

```
Slide [number]: [Slide title]
Section: [Which webinar section this belongs to]
Visual Direction: [What the slide should look like — layout, imagery, data viz, icon]
Headline / Title on slide: [Exact text]
Body content on slide: [Key points — maximum 3 bullets or 1 stat or 1 quote per slide]
Speaker note summary: [1–2 sentences on what the presenter says on this slide]
```

Design rules for the slide brief:
- Reference the client's brand colours and design language from earlier outputs
- No wall-of-text slides. One idea per slide.
- Data and stats get their own slides with large typography
- Case study moments get a dedicated slide with outcome callout
- Every 5–7 slides, include an engagement or transition slide to reset attention
- Use visual formats native to the industry where they aid comprehension — product shots, diagrams, before/after imagery, floor plans, process flows, spec comparisons, charts

### STEP 6 — REGISTRATION PAGE COPY (if requested in inputs)

If Registration Page = Yes, write the complete registration page:

- **Page Headline (H1):** Matches webinar title — specific, outcome-led
- **Subheadline:** Expands the big promise — who this is for and what they'll get
- **What You'll Learn:** 4–6 specific bullet points (outcomes, not vague topics)
- **Who This Is For:** 3–4 lines describing the exact ICP — make them self-select
- **Who This Is NOT For:** 1–2 lines (helps qualify and increases curiosity)
- **About the Presenter:** Short, proof-led bio
- **Social Proof:** 1–2 testimonials from people who match the ICP (or credibility statement if no testimonials are available)
- **Registration Form:** Field labels and CTA button copy — request only the fields the client genuinely needs for this business type
- **FAQ:** 3–4 short objection-handling questions (Is this free? How long? Will it be recorded? Do I need to prepare anything?)
- **Urgency Element:** Genuine reason to register now (limited seats, one-time live, replay available for limited time only — use only what is true)
- **Compliance/consent line** where the region or industry requires it (marketing consent, data handling, professional disclaimer)

### STEP 7 — EMAIL SEQUENCE (if requested in inputs)

If Follow-Up Emails = Yes, write the following in full:

**Pre-Webinar Sequence (3 emails):**

**Email 1 — Registration Confirmation (Immediate)**
Subject: You're in — here's what to expect on [date/time]
Goal: Confirm registration, deliver anticipation, set expectations, give them one thing to think about before the webinar.

**Email 2 — Value Primer (2 days before)**
Subject: Before [Webinar Title] — one thing worth knowing
Goal: Deliver one specific insight related to the webinar topic. Remind them why they registered. Build anticipation. This is not a reminder email — it is a value email that happens to remind them at the end.

**Email 3 — Day-Of Reminder (morning of webinar)**
Subject: Starting today at [time] — your link is inside
Goal: Deliver the join link, remind them of the big promise, and give them a practical reason to show up live vs. watching the replay (e.g. live Q&A, live-only offer, real-time examples).

**Post-Webinar Sequence (3 emails):**

**Email 4 — Immediate Follow-Up (within 1 hour of webinar ending)**
Subject: The replay + what we covered today
Goal: Thank them for attending, deliver the replay link (if applicable), summarise the 3 biggest takeaways, soft CTA to the conversion goal.

**Email 5 — Value Reinforcement (Day 2 post-webinar)**
Subject: The one thing most [ICP descriptor] get wrong after [topic]
Goal: Deliver one additional insight not covered in the webinar. Position the CTA as the natural next step for those who want to implement what they learned.

**Email 6 — Final Push (Day 5 post-webinar)**
Subject: Last chance — [offer or deadline]
Goal: Close the loop. Use genuine urgency (replay expiry, offer deadline, limited spots). Restate the single action. Make it easy to say yes.

For each email provide: subject line, preview text, full body copy, CTA copy, and P.S. line.

### STEP 8 — IMPLEMENTATION NOTES

**Webinar Platform Recommendation**
Based on the format (live / pre-recorded / workshop / demo / training) and the client's region, industry, and likely tech stack established in this conversation, recommend the most suitable platform with a one-paragraph rationale. Consider audience expectations — a B2B enterprise audience, a clinical or professional-development audience, and a consumer audience have different norms around registration friction, CPD/CE tracking, and replay access.

**Promotion Checklist**
List the channels and assets needed to fill the webinar. Select what is realistic for this industry and audience, which may include:
- Organic social posts (platforms, frequency, content angle)
- Paid promotion where relevant to the audience
- Email broadcast to existing list or customer database
- Industry associations, professional bodies, trade publications, or CPD/CE listings
- Partner, distributor, reseller, or referral promotion opportunities
- Community, group, forum, or event-adjacent promotion
- Internal CTA placement (pillar page, funnel pages, product pages, email signatures, packaging inserts, in-store or on-site signage)

**KPIs & Benchmarks**
- Registration rate target
- Attendance rate target (live show-up rate)
- Engagement benchmarks (poll participation, Q&A volume)
- CTA conversion rate (registrants who take the primary action)
- Replay view rate (if applicable)

**CRO Chemical Layer Map**
Table mapping each webinar section against the 5 psychological layers: Dopamine / Oxytocin / Serotonin / Endorphin / Adrenaline — rated HIGH / MED / LOW.
