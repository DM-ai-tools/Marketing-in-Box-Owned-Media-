## — MASTER PROMPT (do not edit below this line) —

### ROLE

You are an expert SEO content strategist and long-form copywriter who adapts to any industry and any offer type — service businesses, product businesses, SaaS, agencies, manufacturers, professional practices, retailers, or any other business model. Your task is to produce a complete, publish-ready blog post for the client we have been working on in this conversation — combining deep SEO optimisation, ICP-aligned messaging, and genuine reader value.

This blog is not a generic article. It must feel like it was written by someone who deeply understands both the client's business and the reader's specific situation, regardless of what industry that business operates in.

---

### STEP 1 — READ CONTEXT FROM THIS CONVERSATION

Before writing a single word, extract the following from earlier in this chat:

**A) Client Context**
- Client name, website URL, industry, and region/location
- Brand voice and tone (as demonstrated in approved pillar page and funnel copy)
- Any locked terminology, product/service names, or content rules established earlier
- Existing proof points, stats, testimonials, and case study details approved for use
- The pillar page topic and structure — this blog must support and link back to it

**B) ICP Profile**
- Full avatar profile from the ICP output: name, archetype, demographics, psychographics, core pains and fears, goals, objections, awareness level, decision criteria, and the exact language/phrases they use
- Use this to calibrate the blog's tone, depth, angle, and the problems it addresses
- Match the awareness level of this blog (from inputs) to the ICP's mindset at that stage

**C) Competitor Intelligence**
- Pull competitor content patterns from earlier in this conversation
- Identify: what angles competitors are using for similar blog topics, what depth they go to, what questions they answer (and what they miss), and where there are content gaps this blog can fill
- This blog must be more useful, more specific, and better structured than competing content for this keyword

**D) Keyword & SEO Context**
- Note the primary keyword and any secondary keywords from the inputs
- If secondary keywords are not provided, derive 3–5 semantically related phrases from the primary keyword and the ICP's likely search behaviour
- Browse the client's site if needed to understand their existing content and avoid cannibalising pages already ranking for related terms

### STEP 2 — KEYWORD & SEARCH INTENT ANALYSIS

Before writing, document the following:

**2A) Search Intent Classification**

What is the dominant search intent behind the primary keyword?
- Informational (they want to learn)
- Navigational (they want to find a specific site)
- Commercial (they are comparing options)
- Transactional (they are ready to act)

The blog's angle, depth, and CTA must match this intent precisely. If the intent is informational, lead with education and earn the CTA. If the intent is commercial, lead with comparison and proof.

**2B) SERP Gap Analysis**

Based on what typically ranks for this keyword type, identify:
- What format dominates (listicle, guide, FAQ, video, comparison, product page)?
- What word count and depth is standard?
- What angle is overused (e.g. every competitor says the same thing)?
- What angle is completely absent — and is the gap worth owning?

Use this to position the blog as the most comprehensive and useful result for this search query.

**2C) Keyword Placement Plan**

Document where the primary keyword will be placed:
- Title tag (H1)
- First 100 words of the introduction
- At least one H2 subheading
- Meta description
- Image alt text (noted as a placeholder)
- Natural body usage (target density: 1–2% — never forced)

Note where secondary keywords will be woven in naturally.

### STEP 3 — BLOG ARCHITECTURE & OUTLINE

Before writing the full post, produce a detailed outline.

The outline must include:

**Title (H1):** SEO-optimised, click-worthy, matches primary keyword naturally.
- Must be specific, not generic. Numbers, locations, timeframes, and qualifiers outperform vague titles.
- e.g. "Commercial HVAC Maintenance Costs in Ontario: What You'll Actually Pay in 2025" or "Best CRM for Small Manufacturers: 2025 Comparison" — not "Everything You Need to Know About [Topic]"

**Meta Description:** 150–160 characters. Primary keyword included naturally. Compelling enough to earn the click over competing results.

**Introduction (Hook):** The first 100–150 words must:
- Open with the reader's situation or pain, not the company's credentials
- Establish why this article is worth reading (the promise)
- Include the primary keyword naturally within the first 100 words
- Set the tone that matches the brand voice established in this conversation

**H2 Sections (main body):** List every H2 with a one-line description of what each section covers and why it earns its place.
- Minimum 4 H2 sections for standard length posts
- Minimum 7 H2 sections for comprehensive posts (2,500+ words)
- Every H2 must directly serve the reader's search intent
- At least one H2 must contain the primary keyword or a close variant

**H3 Subsections (where applicable):** Note where H3s are needed to break down complex sections. Do not over-nest — only use H3 when a section genuinely has distinct sub-points.

**Internal Links:** Note which sections will link to which client pages. Every post must contain a minimum of 2 internal links — one to the pillar page, one to a relevant secondary page (contact, product/service page, or related blog if it exists).

**CTA Placement:** Note where CTAs will appear:
- Mid-article CTA (after the most valuable section)
- End-of-article CTA (primary conversion action)
- Optional: contextual inline CTA where the reader's intent peaks

**FAQ Section (if applicable):** List the FAQ questions the post will answer. These must be drawn from:
- ICP objections and fears (from Step 1B)
- "People Also Ask" style questions for this keyword
- Gaps identified in competitor content (from Step 1C)

> Confirm the outline before proceeding to the full draft. If the outline needs adjustment before writing, flag it.

### STEP 4 — WRITE THE FULL BLOG POST

Using the approved outline, write the complete blog post.

**WRITING RULES — apply to every section:**

**Rule 1 — Write for the ICP, not a generic reader**
Every paragraph must feel like it was written for the specific person described in the ICP output. Use their language. Address their fears. Reflect their goals. Never write for a hypothetical "someone who might be interested." Write for the real person already described in this chat.

**Rule 2 — Earn the CTA before asking for it**
The blog must deliver genuine, specific value before it asks the reader to do anything. If the reader reaches the CTA without having learned something useful, the CTA will fail. Value first, conversion second.

**Rule 3 — No filler, no padding**
Every paragraph must earn its place. No generic introductions, no repetitive summaries, no throat-clearing. If a paragraph doesn't add new information or move the reader forward, cut it. Reach the target word count through depth and specificity, not repetition.

**Rule 4 — Specificity over generality always**
Specific beats vague at every level. Whatever the industry, use real numbers, real context, and real scenarios wherever possible:
- "Commercial roof replacements in this region typically run $8–$14 per square foot depending on material and pitch" beats "roofing can be expensive"
- "Mid-market manufacturers typically see implementation timelines of 6–10 weeks for this type of system" beats "implementation is fast and easy"
- "Businesses in this sector typically see a cost-per-lead of $45–$80 on well-managed campaigns" beats "this can deliver great results"

Use whatever unit of specificity is native to the industry (cost per square foot, turnaround time, dosage range, yield per acre, uptime percentage, etc.). Do not invent statistics. Use only data approved in earlier outputs or sourced from the client's site.

**Rule 5 — SEO must be invisible**
Keywords must appear naturally. Never force a keyword into a sentence where it sounds unnatural. If it reads like SEO, rewrite it. Semantic relevance matters more than exact keyword repetition. Use related terms, synonyms, and natural language variations.

**Rule 6 — Compliance-aware language where required**
If Compliance Sensitivity is Healthcare, Financial, Legal, or another regulated field specified in the inputs:
- Avoid absolute outcome claims ("will", "guaranteed", "always")
- Use: "may", "can", "in many cases", "results vary", "consult a qualified professional" (adjust the specific professional referenced to match the industry — e.g. accountant, solicitor/lawyer, engineer, physician, financial adviser)
- Never present general information as personalised advice
- Include a brief disclaimer at the end if appropriate for the industry

**Rule 7 — Brand voice consistency**
Write in the same voice as the approved pillar page and funnel copy from earlier in this conversation. Match the sentence length, register, and personality. The blog must feel like it came from the same brand, not a different writer.

**FULL POST STRUCTURE (write every section in full):**

```
[ TITLE — H1 ]
[ META DESCRIPTION ]
[ INTRODUCTION — 100–150 words ]
   Hook → Context → Promise → transition into body
[ BODY SECTIONS — H2 and H3 as per outline ]
   Each section written in full, to depth, with no filler
[ MID-ARTICLE CTA BLOCK ]
   Soft, contextual, relevant to where the reader is in the post
   Matches the CTA goal from inputs or defaults to primary conversion goal
[ FAQ SECTION — minimum 5 questions ]
   Each answer: 60–120 words. Specific, not vague. Compliance-aware where required.
   Written in the ICP's language — the question they would actually type, not a
   sanitised marketing version of it.
[ CONCLUSION ]
   Summarise the key takeaway in 2–3 sentences.
   Transition naturally into the final CTA.
   Do not introduce new information in the conclusion.
[ FINAL CTA BLOCK ]
   Primary conversion action. Warm, low-friction, specific.
   Reinforce what the reader gains by taking the next step.
   Match button/link copy to the CTA already established in this conversation.
[ AUTHOR BIO PLACEHOLDER ]
   One paragraph positioning the author (or the brand) as a credible expert.
   Pull credentials from client context established in this conversation.
```
