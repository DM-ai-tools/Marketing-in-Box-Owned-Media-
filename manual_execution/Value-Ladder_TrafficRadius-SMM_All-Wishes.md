## STEP 0 — CONTEXT RECONCILED

**A) Client identity.** TrafficRadius is Melbourne-based but services clients Australia-wide (per the rewritten pillar page). No geographic serviceability restriction applies to digital-delivery offers; only in-person/local-event formats are Melbourne-bound.

**B) ICP.** Danielle "Dani" Whitlock — Head of Marketing, 60-person Melbourne professional services (accounting/advisory) firm. Problem-aware, advanced maturity, buying diagnosis not delivery. Scarcest resource: partner time, not budget. Every "Solves" line below names a pain drawn directly from her profile: the four causes (channel-fit / brand-architecture / operating-model / measurement), partner-time scarcity, compliance sensitivity, attribution blindness in long consideration cycles, and the fear of spending internal credibility on a failed initiative. Where an offer serves TrafficRadius's broader client base (trades, hospitality, retail — per the pillar page's industry cards), that's noted, but the primary buyer modelled throughout is Dani.

**C) CRO / messaging framework.** Reused verbatim throughout: the two locked CTA labels (**Book a Social Media Marketing Strategy Consultation** / **Get a Free Social Media Audit**), the four-cause diagnostic framework, the "flat line in an otherwise healthy funnel" hook, the no-lock-in/ownership commitments, and the compliance-aware, no-guarantee tone established in the pillar page and CRO audit.

**D) Competitor analysis** (`Offers_Competitors_TR_SMM.xlsx`, 10 AU competitors). Market pricing clusters into three bands: **budget** ($480–$720/month, Hatchify — templated, post-count-based, DIY-adjacent), **mid-market** ($1,000–$1,500/month, Swoop, BeKonstructive, Catalyst — tiered packages, transparent inclusions), and **full-funnel** ($2,500/month, SOUP, Bambrick — bundled with SEO/Ads). Every competitor in this set sells a **package-first** offer — pick a tier, get inclusions. **Not one competitor sells a diagnosis-first offer, a productised low-ticket entry point, or a capacity-constrained premium tier.** That gap is exploited throughout this ladder: TrafficRadius's Rung 2 sits deliberately above the mid-market band (justified by the diagnostic differentiation established on the pillar page), and Rungs 0–1 claim the productised-entry territory nobody else occupies.

**E) Existing offers already in market.** Per the live site and the funnel already built in this engagement, TrafficRadius currently has exactly two productised touchpoints: the **Free Social Media Audit** (Rung 0, already live) and the **Social Media Marketing Strategy Consultation** (a free, bounded diagnostic call — functionally Rung 0/1 boundary, already live). Beyond that, the core service is sold as a **custom, unpriced quote** — no published packages, no tiers. This ladder treats the Audit and Consultation as already-occupied and does not duplicate them; new Rung 0 items are additive lead magnets, and Rung 2 onward is where the currently-unproductised custom quote gets structured into real rungs.

**F) Financial anchor.** No annual outcome value or budget norm was supplied. **Assumption stated per instructions:** using the ICP's own example — a single partner-generated LinkedIn post produced a pipeline succession engagement valued at **$40,000–$60,000** — and assuming a properly functioning social channel plausibly sources one to two such engagements per year, the **annual value of the outcome to this customer is estimated at $100,000 AUD** (midpoint). Rung 3 is set at 10–20% of that anchor (**$10,000–$20,000**, representative sticker **$15,000**), cross-checked against the competitor ceiling of $2,500/month ($30,000/year) — TrafficRadius's high-ticket rung sits above the market's current top band, which is defensible given the diagnostic positioning, but is flagged for the client to sanity-check against real close rates once sold.
