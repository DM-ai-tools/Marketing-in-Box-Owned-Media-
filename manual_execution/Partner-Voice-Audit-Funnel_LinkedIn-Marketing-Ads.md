# PART 1 — FUNNEL STRATEGY & ARCHITECTURE

## 1A. Strategic Rationale

Dani's relationship to LinkedIn specifically is more advanced than her relationship to social media in general. By the time a visitor reaches the LinkedIn Marketing & Ads page, they've already decided LinkedIn is worth a dedicated look — they're past "does social work for us" and into "why isn't LinkedIn working the way it should." That's a narrower, more specific question than the one the Four Causes Scorecard answers, and it needs a narrower, more specific instrument.

**Why the Partner Voice Audit, specifically, at this stage:**
The LinkedIn pillar page's own Section 3 ("The Company Page Trap") already names the mechanism: LinkedIn's distribution favours individual profiles over company pages, and most professional services firms are publishing into the wrong one. Dani's ICP profile describes this exact pattern happening to her personally — *"Michael posts something off the cuff from an airport and gets more traction than our whole content calendar. What does that tell me?"* She has lived the symptom. What she hasn't done is measure it. The Partner Voice Audit converts a vague, uncomfortable feeling ("I think our content is too company-led") into a specific number she can act on — which matches her decision criteria directly: ICP §10 states she needs *"a tangible artefact she can take to the partner group"* and that she is buying *"clarity, not delivery."*

**Why this doesn't duplicate the Four Causes Scorecard:**
The Plan of Action is explicit that this must be "distinct format and mechanic... does not duplicate the existing lead magnet." The Four Causes Scorecard diagnoses across four possible causes at the level of the whole social media function. The Partner Voice Audit does one thing only, one level deeper: it measures the brand-architecture cause specifically, on LinkedIn specifically, with enough granularity to tell her not just "this is your cause" but roughly how far along the spectrum her firm sits and what a realistic target looks like.

**The competitive gap this exploits (detailed in Part 2):**
No interactive diagnostic tool of any kind exists anywhere in the ten-competitor SMM lead magnet set already researched in this engagement, and none of the ten broader agency competitors identified in the Plan of Action's competitor comparison maintain a dedicated LinkedIn-only service page at all — meaning none could plausibly have built a LinkedIn-specific diagnostic either. This is genuinely unclaimed territory, not a marginal improvement on an existing format.

**Connection to the pillar page and existing funnel architecture:**
This funnel does not introduce new claims or a new CTA. It reuses the LinkedIn pillar page's exact language (the "company page trap," the four specific symptoms in Section 3, the capture-and-amplify mechanic, the locked CTA), and it ascends to the same **Social Media Marketing Strategy Consultation** the rest of the site already runs on — just labelled locally as a "LinkedIn Strategy Session," exactly as the Plan of Action specifies and exactly as the main Social Media Marketing Funnel document already established the pattern of channel-specific relabelling of one underlying service.

**What's present:** phone-call-first consultations (BAMBRICK, Digital Next, Web Marketing Experts), a handful of gated documents (Social Status's ebook, Missing Link's checklist hub), and one quantified free audit offer (Online Marketing Gurus).

**What's conspicuously absent, specific to this funnel's exact territory:**

## 2C. Competitive Value Ladder (typical, as observed in this market)


As with the main Social Media Marketing Funnel's finding, the entry rung in this category is short and undifferentiated — and for LinkedIn specifically, there effectively is no entry rung at all, because no competitor has built a dedicated LinkedIn funnel to have a rung on.

### Section 2: The 10 Questions
**CRO Trigger:** Serotonin (question specificity signals real category expertise) + Dopamine (each answer moves the visible progress bar, a small reward loop)

**Design note:** present one question per screen/card, with the progress bar advancing after each answer. Each question is multiple-choice (4 options), scored 0–3 per answer (0 = most company-led, 3 = most partner-led). Total possible score: 30, converted to a 0–100 Partner Voice Score for the reveal.

**Q1.** In the last three months, what share of your firm's LinkedIn posts were published from the company page versus an individual's personal profile?
- Almost entirely the company page *(0)*
- Mostly the company page, occasional individual posts *(1)*
- Roughly even split *(2)*
- Mostly individual profiles, company page supports them *(3)*

**Q2.** When did a partner or principal at your firm last post something on their own LinkedIn profile about their work?
- Can't recall, or never *(0)*
- More than three months ago *(1)*
- Within the last month *(2)*
- Within the last week, and it's a regular habit *(3)*

**Q3.** Compare a typical rough, unscripted post from a partner against your designed company-page content. Which gets more genuine engagement — comments and DMs, not just impressions?
- We haven't seen this happen, or the company page always wins *(0)*
- Roughly the same *(1)*
- The individual post, noticeably *(2)*
- The individual post, by a wide margin — we've seen this repeatedly *(3)*

**Q4.** Do you currently run paid LinkedIn campaigns built around a named individual's content (Thought Leader Ads), or only sponsored content from the company page?
- Only company-page sponsored content, or no paid LinkedIn activity *(0)*
- We've tested it once or twice, informally *(1)*
- We run it occasionally, without a defined process *(2)*
- We run Thought Leader Ads as a standing part of our program *(3)*

**Q5.** How many of your senior people (partners, principals, practice leads) have a complete, active LinkedIn profile they post from at least monthly?
- None *(0)*
- One *(1)*
- Two or three *(2)*
- Four or more, or a defined rotating group *(3)*

**Q6.** Do you have a defined process for turning what a partner already says well in client meetings into published content — a "capture" process?
- No process — content is written from scratch, separately *(0)*
- Informal, ad hoc, depends who's available *(1)*
- Semi-formal — someone occasionally records or notes what a partner says *(2)*
- Yes, a defined, repeatable capture process exists *(3)*

**Q7.** How long does content typically take to clear internal review or compliance before it can be published?
- More than a week *(0)*
- Three to seven days *(1)*
- One to two days *(2)*
- Same day, or boundaries are pre-agreed so review is rarely a blocker *(3)*

**Q8.** Do you track which specific LinkedIn posts — company or individual — lead to actual enquiries, or only engagement metrics like likes and impressions?
- Only engagement metrics *(0)*
- We look at engagement and guess at the rest *(1)*
- We have some attribution, but it's inconsistent *(2)*
- We track this specifically, including self-reported attribution on enquiry forms *(3)*

**Q9.** Have you activated employee advocacy — making it easy for willing staff to share or engage with company content from their own profiles?
- No, and we haven't considered it *(0)*
- We've discussed it, nothing built *(1)*
- A few staff do this informally *(2)*
- Yes, it's a defined, opt-in program *(3)*

**Q10.** If a client said, "I've been seeing your firm a lot on LinkedIn lately," could you confidently say whose face they'd associate with that — or just your logo?
- Just the logo *(0)*
- Probably the logo, maybe one person *(1)*
- One or two specific people, most likely *(2)*
- Several specific people, clearly *(3)*
