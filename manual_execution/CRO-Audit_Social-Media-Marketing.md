# CRO Audit — trafficradius.com.au/social-media-marketing/

**Audited:** 30 July 2026
**Framework:** Conversion goal clarity → 5 psychological layers → mandatory ingredients → 12-section architecture
**Primary conversion goal (as it should be):** Book a strategy call / Request a free proposal
**Secondary:** Get a free audit

**Overall score: 58/100** — Strong raw material, weak conversion discipline. The page has more content than most competitors but leaks trust through copy-paste errors and offers a single undifferentiated CTA destination.

---

## 0. Conversion goal clarity — 4/10

| Check | Finding |
|---|---|
| One dominant goal | ✗ Three competing asks: "Get a free audit" form, "Get Started", and 10+ differently-worded CTA buttons |
| CTA destination | ✗ Almost every CTA dumps to `/contact-us/` — a generic form, not a booking flow |
| Goal matches intent | ✗ "Free audit" is the loudest offer; a strategy call is the actual sale. The framework's priority order is inverted |

**Critical issues:**

1. **Page title and meta are for the wrong service.** `<title>` renders as *"Elevate Your Business with Expert Local SEO Services Today!"*, og:title and og:description both sell Local SEO. Google is being told this is an SEO page. This alone suppresses clicks and rankings for social media terms.
2. **Broken CTA:** "Get a Free Strategy Call" in the industries section links to `#` — dead button on the highest-intent micro-moment on the page.
3. **Wrong-service CTA:** "Schedule Your Free **SEO** Consultation" appears after the social media benefits block.
4. **CTA label chaos:** "get your audit now!", "request your review now!", "get your analysis today!", "request your demo today!", "book your consult today". Nine different promises = nine different mental models of what happens next. Pick two.

## 1. Dopamine — Attention + Desire — 6/10

**Working:**
- H1 + subhead pairing is correct in structure ("Social Media Marketing Agency" / "Transform Your Social Presence into Real Business Growth")
- "not just likes and follows" is a genuinely good hook — it names the thing buyers resent
- Interactive goal-selector ("I need more leads / traffic / customers…") is a strong dopamine device: it makes the visitor self-declare intent

**Gaps:**
- **The 400% / 250% claim is unsubstantiated and generic.** "Average 400% increase in audience reach" reads as invented. Reach is also a vanity metric — using it in the hero contradicts the "not just likes" promise two lines above. Self-cancelling.
- No specificity in the hero. No named client, no dollar figure, no timeframe.
- Hero has no visible "book a call" button — only the audit form. The desire generated has nowhere fast to go.

**Fix priority:** Replace the two percentages with one verifiable, attributable outcome. Add a secondary text CTA under the form.

## 2. Oxytocin — Trust + Connection — 4/10

**Working:**
- 20-logo client wall, recognisable AU names (The Good Guys, Koala Living, Hello Hello Plants)
- Industry blocks (Trades, Hospitality, Education & Childcare, Fitness) show real context awareness
- Two case studies with named verticals and specific tactics

**Gaps — this is the weakest layer:**
- **"Default Text" is printed above all five testimonials.** A live placeholder string. Instantly reads as unfinished.
- **Testimonials are anonymous and unverifiable.** "Emily R., Boutique Hotel Manager" with no company, no photo, no logo, no link. First-initial-only testimonials are widely read as fabricated. You have 20 real client logos — the mismatch between the logo wall and the anonymous quotes actively creates suspicion.
- **Case studies are unnamed** ("Boutique Fitness Chain In Sydney", "New Homeware Line Melbourne") while carrying precise figures (220%, 7.3x ROAS, 3,800 followers). Precise numbers + anonymous source = lowest-credibility combination.
- **No people.** Zero team faces, names, or credentials anywhere on the page. Nothing to connect to.
- **Copy-paste contamination breaks the connection outright:**
  - Services intro reads: *"Our SEO experts for the **fashion industry** assist brands that require strategies tailored to competitive retail and seasonal trends. We help you dominate style searches…"* — wrong service, wrong industry, in the intro paragraph of the main services block.
  - Step 1 of the process: *"As part of our social media marketing services in **Wollongong**…"* — a location the page never otherwise mentions.
  - Comparison table contains leftover rows: *"Review & trust optimisation for **fashion buyers**"*, *"Clear ROI tied to sales & **add-to-cart**"*, *"No **eCommerce** tracking setup"*.
  - Section heading near the footer: *"**SEO** Expertise Across Australia"* above social media body copy.
  - Image alt text throughout references Local SEO, organic search traffic, and Meta Ads dashboards regardless of section.

**These are not typos. To a buyer evaluating whether you'll be careful with their brand, they are the single most damaging thing on the page.**

## 3. Serotonin — Authority + Certainty — 8/10

**Working — strongest layer:**
- 7-step process, sequential and named, each with a deliverable
- 8 service pillars each with a "We deliver:" bullet list — genuinely good scope clarity
- Analytics/reporting treated as its own pillar rather than a footnote
- Platform coverage is complete and each platform gets its own rationale
- Comparison table (Us vs Typical Agencies vs In-House) is well-structured and does real differentiation work

**Gaps:**
- **No expertise credentials.** No Meta Business Partner badge, no Google Partner status, no certifications, no years-in-business, no team size, no "campaigns managed" or "ad spend managed" figure. The process implies competence but nothing evidences it.
- No named strategist or case-study attribution to a person.
- 8 pillars × 6 bullets creates a wall. No prioritisation of what most clients actually buy.
- The comparison table renders twice (desktop + mobile variants both in DOM), with different rows — visible inconsistency risk.

## 4. Endorphin — Safety + Risk Reduction — 6/10

**Working:**
- 12-question FAQ is the best-performing section on the page. It handles real objections directly: time-to-results, who makes the content, organic vs paid, in-house vs agency, tracking and attribution
- "You'll approve key assets, then we handle scheduling" — good control signal
- Reporting cadence mentioned in Step 6
- FAQ closes with an explicit "how to get started" answer

**Gaps:**
- **No pricing signal of any kind.** Not even a starting range or "typical engagements from $X/month". This is the #1 unspoken objection on every agency service page and it goes unanswered.
- **No contract terms.** Lock-in? Month-to-month? Minimum term? Exit terms? Silence reads as risk.
- **No "what happens in the first 30 days"** section. The framework calls for implementation clarity; the 7-step process is chronological but has no timeline attached to it.
- **No ad-spend ownership statement.** Who holds the ad account, who owns the pixel and creative if the relationship ends. Sophisticated buyers ask this first.
- **No form-friction reduction.** The audit form has no "no obligation", "no sales pitch", "we'll respond within 1 business day", or privacy reassurance next to it.
- FAQ sits at the very bottom, below every CTA. Objections are answered after the ask.

---

## 5. Adrenaline — Urgency + Action — 5/10

**Working:**
- CTAs are genuinely repeated (10+ instances) — the framework's repetition requirement is met on volume
- Mid-page audit block specifies what you get: 30-min strategy call, in-depth audit, growth roadmap. This is the best CTA on the page.
- Phone number is sticky in the header — good low-friction path

**Gaps:**
- **Zero urgency mechanism.** No capacity limit, no response-time commitment, no seasonal or cost-of-delay framing. The framework asks for urgency without hype; there is currently neither.
- **No decision-oriented offer tiering.** One undifferentiated "contact us" for a visitor who wants a quick question answered, a visitor who wants a quote, and a visitor ready to book.
- **No cost-of-inaction framing** anywhere — no "every month without tracking is spend you can't attribute".
- **Final CTA is weak.** The page ends on the FAQ accordion with no closing CTA block after it.
- Repetition without variation in *offer* is just noise. Ten buttons all going to `/contact-us/` is one CTA shown ten times.

2. Delete the "Default Text" strings above all five testimonials
3. Rewrite the services intro paragraph — remove the fashion-industry SEO copy
4. Remove "Wollongong" from Step 1
5. Fix the dead `#` link on "Get a Free Strategy Call"

7. Delete the three leftover eCommerce/fashion rows from the comparison table; deduplicate the table
8. Change "SEO Expertise Across Australia" → "Social Media Expertise Across Australia"
9. Rewrite image alt text to match each section

### Ship this month (conversion lift)
10. Add the problem→solution section (Architecture #3) directly under the trust bar
11. Rebuild social proof: name the clients, add company + logo + role, or drop the testimonials and lead with the two case studies attributed properly
12. Add a trust bar: partner badges, Google review rating, years in business, ad spend managed
13. Collapse 10 CTA labels into 2: **"Book a Free Strategy Call"** (primary) and **"Get a Free Social Media Audit"** (secondary)
14. Add a final CTA block after the FAQ
15. Add friction-reduction microcopy at every form: no obligation, no lock-in, response within one business day
16. Reorder: move Benefits (#5) above Services (#6); move FAQ above the final CTA

### Ship this quarter (differentiation)
17. Add pricing transparency — engagement tiers or a starting-from figure
18. Add a "Your first 30 days" timeline section
19. Add an ownership + transparency statement: you own the ad account, the pixel, the creative
20. Add one urgency mechanism — onboarding capacity per month, or a defined response SLA
21. Replace the hero percentages with one attributed client result
