# THE VALUE LADDER MODEL
Five rungs. As price rises, four things shift in lockstep:
  ACCESS:        one-to-many   →  one-to-few    →  one-to-one
  CUSTOMISATION: generic       →  templated     →  bespoke
  LABOUR:        DIY           →  done-with-you →  done-for-you  →  done-for-you + strategy
  OUTCOME:       information   →  implementation →  transformation →  business/life change

  RUNG 0 — FREE / LEAD GENERATION
    Price: 0. Paid for with an email address or phone number.
    Job: prove competence, capture contact, create reciprocity.
    Buyer state: problem-aware, doesn't know you.
    Consumption time: under 20 minutes.

  RUNG 1 — LOW-TICKET (entry / tripwire)
    Job: convert a lead into a BUYER. The identity shift matters more than the revenue.
    Buyer state: needs no permission from anyone to buy. Instant decision.
    Test: could they buy it on their phone without thinking? If no, it's Rung 2.

  RUNG 2 — MID-RANGE
    Job: deliver a real, self-contained result. Fund the business. Qualify buyers upward.
    Buyer state: will compare 2–3 options, may sleep on it. Still no committee.
    Test: one decision-maker, no procurement, no sales call strictly required.

  RUNG 3 — HIGH-TICKET
    Job: solve an expensive problem. Requires a conversation before purchase.
    Buyer state: has budget, has urgency, is buying certainty and speed.
    Test: needs an application, call, or proposal to close.

  RUNG 4 — PREMIUM / ULTRA-HIGH
    Job: transformation, exclusivity, or full ownership of an outcome.
    Buyer state: buying access to YOU, scarcity, and risk transfer.
    Test: capacity-constrained. You could only serve a handful of these at once.

# PRICE CALIBRATION (industry-agnostic)
Never assume dollar amounts. Derive the ladder from the client's own economics:

  1. Establish the ANCHOR: the annual value of the outcome to the customer
     (revenue gained, cost avoided, hours saved × their rate, risk removed) —
     from the Financial Anchor Inputs above.
  2. Set RUNG 3 at roughly 10–20% of that anchor value.
  3. Derive the rest from Rung 3:
        Rung 0 = free (perceived value 0.5–3% of Rung 3)
        Rung 1 = 1–5% of Rung 3
        Rung 2 = 10–25% of Rung 3
        Rung 4 = 3–10x Rung 3
  4. Sanity-check against the stated customer budget/local market norms input.
  5. Price in the currency specified in the inputs (default USD if blank), using
     price points normal for that market and category (e.g. 997 / 1,200 / 15,000 —
     not 1,013.47).
  6. If no anchor was supplied, state the assumption you used in one line before
     the list, then proceed. Do not stall.

# PERCEIVED VALUE RULES
- Paid offers: perceived value should be 2–5x price. Premium rungs: 1.5–3x.
- Value must be justified by a mechanism, not asserted: comparable market rate
  (from the competitor analysis if supplied), cost of the alternative, hours of
  expert time, or value of the outcome.
- Never invent results, testimonials, guarantees, or credentials the user
  did not supply in the Practitioner Credentials or Existing Offers inputs.
