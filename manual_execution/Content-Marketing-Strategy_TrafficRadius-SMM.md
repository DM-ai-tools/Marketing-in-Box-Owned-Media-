### 2.2 What Each Competitor Does Exceptionally Well

**First Page Australia** — Melbourne-based, Google Premier Partner; broadest full-service scope (SEO, PPC, Social, Content, Reputation Management), giving them the most cross-sell surface area of the five but no visible SMM-specific educational depth beyond the single service page.

**Online Marketing Gurus** — The only competitor of the five that splits organic and paid social into two separate URLs (`/social-media-marketing/` and `/social-media-ads/`), mirroring exactly the Meta Ads sub-service split this plan's Phase 2 already committed to — validating that decision rather than contradicting it.

**SIXGUN** — Richmond-based, comparable scope and size to Traffic Radius; positions SEO, SEM/PPC, and Social as three co-equal core lines, suggesting a generalist rather than SMM-authority positioning — the gap this plan is built to close.

**nimbl** — Boutique, SMB-focused positioning closest to Traffic Radius's own target segment, but no visible interactive tools, benchmark data, or professional-services-specific content.

### 2.3 Identified Content Gaps (Traffic Radius Opportunities)

**Gap 1 — Professional Services / LinkedIn Specificity.** None of the five competitors show visible content built around the individual-vs-company-page mechanic that matters most for professional services buyers. This is the exact gap the LinkedIn Marketing & Ads sub-service (already scoped in the Plan of Action) is designed to own, and Cluster A/B content below is built to reinforce it.

**Gap 2 — Interactive Diagnostic Tools.** Zero of the five competitors offer any interactive scoring tool, calculator, or diagnostic — the same whitespace finding that led to the Four Causes Scorecard. This plan's signature asset and two of its cluster tools extend that advantage rather than treating it as a one-off.

**Gap 3 — Transparent, AUD-Specific Cost Content.** No competitor publishes plain-English pricing or cost-driver content; several use "custom quote" positioning identical to Traffic Radius's own. This is not a gap to imitate blindly (Traffic Radius's own FAQ deliberately keeps quote-after-conversation positioning) but there is room to publish an honest "what drives the cost" educational piece without publishing a fixed price list — Cluster A content below does this.

**Gap 4 — Industry-Vertical Depth.** All five competitors serve "any SMB" with no vertical-specific content. Traffic Radius already has 7 locked industry verticals (Trades, Professional Services, Hospitality, Education & Childcare, Fitness & Wellness, Local Retail & Showrooms, Building & Construction) named on the pillar page but with no supporting content behind any of them — Cluster D exists specifically to close this.

## 3. Target Audience & Buyer Personas

All content must be written for one of three personas, each declared explicitly in every content brief.

### 3.1 Primary Persona — "Diagnostic Dani" (Professional Services Owner/Partner)
| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Role | Owner, Partner, or Head of Marketing at a Melbourne professional services firm (accounting, legal, advisory, engineering, financial planning) |
| Company size | Small-to-mid, partner-led, $2M–$20M AUD revenue band |
| Geography | Melbourne and greater Victoria primary; open to Australia-wide |
| Current state | Running some social media internally or via a generalist agency; can see it "isn't working" but can't name why |
| Content consumption | LinkedIn (passive, months of silent consumption before enquiry), problem-shaped Google searches, industry podcasts on the commute |
| Pain point | "I can't name the problem precisely enough to buy a solution — everything I can see is a symptom." |
| Decision trigger | A diagnostic that names the specific cause (channel-fit, brand-architecture, operating-model, or measurement) rather than a generic pitch |
| Preferred format | Short diagnostic tool, a named-peer case study, a 20-minute teardown-style video |

### 3.2 Secondary Persona — "Practice Manager Priya" (Internal Marketing/Practice Coordinator)
| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Role | Marketing Coordinator or Practice Manager reporting to the partners/GM |

| Content consumption | LinkedIn (active), industry association newsletters (CPA Australia, CA ANZ, Accountants Daily), benchmark reports |
| Decision trigger | Content that gives her ammunition for an internal partner-group conversation — benchmarks, frameworks, defensible numbers |
| Preferred format | Benchmark reports, auditable checklists, partner-time-costed frameworks |

### 3.3 Tertiary Persona — "Switching Sam" (Agency-Frustrated Buyer)
| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Role | Business owner or marketing lead currently with another generalist agency |
| Pain point | Results flatlined; social folded into a broader retainer with no dedicated strategy; no partner-time-costed transparency |

## 4. Topic Cluster Architecture

The pillar-and-cluster model tells Google that trafficradius.com.au/social-media-marketing/ is the definitive Australian authority on social media marketing for professional services and owner-operated SMBs, by surrounding it with tightly linked supporting content.

### 4.1 Four Cluster Categories

**Cluster A — Strategy & Planning (Awareness-Stage)**
- Is social media marketing worth it for a professional services firm in Australia?
- Social media marketing benchmarks by industry (2026 Australian edition)
- Social media marketing vs SEO: which should a professional services firm invest in first?
- When social media fails: the four causes behind flat performance

- Setting your first social media strategy: a 30-minute framework for partner-led firms
- Seasonal social media strategy for Australian professional services (EOFY, tax season, year-end)

**Cluster B — Content & Distribution Execution (Consideration-Stage)**
- LinkedIn Thought Leader Ads: a practical guide for accounting and advisory firms
- Getting senior partners to actually contribute content without adding to billable hours
- Writing social media copy that doesn't sound like every other firm's
- Meta Ads vs organic reach: what actually drives enquiries for local service businesses
- Content calendar planning for partner-led businesses: a realistic monthly system
- Measuring engagement quality, not vanity metrics, on LinkedIn
- Compliance-safe social content for regulated-adjacent industries
- Employee/partner advocacy programmes: how to start one without it feeling forced

**Cluster C — Optimisation & Measurement (Decision-Stage)**
- The weekly social media audit: a 12-point checklist
- Why your social media enquiries dropped (a diagnostic walkthrough using the Four Causes framework)
- Dark social: why your best leads aren't in your attribution report
- Social media reporting for non-marketers: what to show your partner group
- Scaling social media from a side-project to a real channel
- Hiring a social media agency: 15 questions to ask
- Attribution for long-consideration-cycle sales: fixing last-click blind spots

**Cluster D — Industry Deep-Dives (Vertical Authority Content — 7 locked verticals)**
- Social media marketing for Australian trades businesses
- Social media marketing for professional services firms (accounting, legal, advisory)
- Social media marketing for hospitality venues
- Social media marketing for education and childcare providers
- Social media marketing for fitness and wellness studios
- Social media marketing for local retail and showrooms
- Social media marketing for building and construction companies

### Interlinking Rule
Every cluster article links up to the pillar once in the intro and once in the conclusion. Every pillar section links down to 1–2 relevant cluster articles. Every cluster article links sideways to 2–3 sibling cluster articles. Non-negotiable — this is how the pillar model functions.

### 10.2 Content Brief Template (Non-Negotiable)

Every piece of content begins with a one-page brief specifying:
1. Primary and secondary keywords with AU search volume (estimated where no data source exists) and difficulty.
2. Primary persona (Dani / Priya / Sam) and stated reader takeaway.
3. Funnel stage (TOFU / MOFU / BOFU) and desired next action.
4. Three competitor articles being outranked, with noted content gaps (or a note that no directly comparable competitor article exists — itself a finding).
5. Interlinking map: 3 pillar links, 2 sibling cluster links, 1 CTA destination (Book a Social Media Marketing Strategy Consultation or Get a Free Social Media Audit — never a new, unestablished CTA).
6. Required H2 structure and featured-snippet target question.
7. Distribution checklist (newsletter slot, LinkedIn post, email sequence insertion).

### 10.3 Quality Checklist (Pre-Publish)
- **Originality:** zero AI-generated boilerplate; all content passes human editorial review.
- **Accuracy:** every AUD benchmark figure sourced from real client data or clearly cited public AU data — no invented statistics.
- **Voice:** Australian spelling, Melbourne/professional-services examples, Traffic Radius's established plainspoken, diagnostic, no-hype tone.
- **Compliance:** ACCC and Australian Consumer Law respected — no misleading claims, no fabricated guarantees, conditional outcome language consistent with every other asset built for this client.
- **Accessibility:** minimum WCAG 2.1 AA; alt text on all images; logical heading structure.

### 10.4 Review & Iteration Cadence
- **Weekly:** editorial huddle, distribution review.
- **Monthly:** content dashboard review, underperformer audit (bottom 10%), cluster rebalance.
- **Quarterly:** pillar page refresh, benchmark report data refresh, persona validation, competitor re-scan.
- **Annually:** full content audit, ROI assessment, strategic redirection for the next 12 months.
