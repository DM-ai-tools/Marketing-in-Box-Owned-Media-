**Client:** TrafficRadius

**Region:** Melbourne, VIC, Australia
**Primary conversion goal:** Converting leads — booked **Social Media Marketing Strategy Consultation**
**ICP:** Danielle "Dani" Whitlock — *"The Metrics-Rich, Signal-Poor Growth Lead."* Head of Marketing & Client Growth, 60-person Melbourne accounting and business advisory firm. **Problem-aware. Advanced maturity. Stuck at diagnosis, not at shortlist.**
**Competitor set:** First Page, Online Marketing Gurus, SIXGUN, SOUP Agency, nimbl, JBE Digital, Global Genie, Softtrix, Neon Treehouse, BeKonstructive (10 domains, similarity 0.75–0.93)
**Execution type:** Quick Win / Spear Gun — expand and strengthen an existing indexed page. Do not replace.

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The page is well above category average on content depth and process clarity. It is scoring poorly on the one thing that decides whether Dani converts: **it sells delivery to a buyer who is shopping for diagnosis.**

**Score: 58/100**

| Layer | Score | One-line verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Conversion goal clarity | 4/10 | Ten CTA labels, one generic destination, no consultation offer named |
| Dopamine (attention/desire) | 6/10 | Good hook, undermined by unattributable vanity stats |
| Oxytocin (trust/connection) | 4/10 | "Default Text" live on page; anonymous testimonials beside 20 real logos |

| **ICP alignment** | **3/10** | **Written for a local SMB owner. Dani is a B2B marketing lead at a professional services firm.** |

**The single highest-leverage finding:** Dani is not searching "social media agency Melbourne." She is searching *"why isn't LinkedIn working for professional services firms."* Every competitor in the set — all ten — enters the conversation at the solution layer. The page currently does the same. There is open whitespace for TrafficRadius to enter at the **diagnostic** layer, and the ICP identifies this as the highest-leverage gap in the segment (ICP §13, Opportunity 1).

## AUDIT SECTION 1 — CONVERSION GOAL CLARITY

### Is the primary conversion goal clear?

No. Three offers compete, and the one that matches the ICP's awareness level is absent entirely.

| Offer on page | Prominence | Fit to Dani |
|---|---|---|
| "GET A FREE AUDIT" (multi-step form) | Highest — hero + mid-page | Partial. She'd take an audit, but "audit" implies she's already decided to outsource. She hasn't. |
| "Get Started" (header) | Persistent | Poor. Premature for a problem-aware buyer. |
| "Contact Us" variants ×10 | Diffuse | Poor. No named deliverable. |
| **Social Media Marketing Strategy Consultation** | **Absent** | **This is the offer she is actually in the market for.** |

The service she would buy is not named anywhere on the page.

### Are CTAs specific and low-friction?

They are frequent but incoherent. Verbatim CTA labels currently live on the page:

1. "Start Your Free Facebook Ads Strategy Session"
2. "book your consult today"
3. "get your audit now!"
4. "schedule your call today!"
5. "request your review now!"
6. "get your analysis today!"
7. "Request your demo or custom strategy report today!"
8. "request your demo today!"
9. "Schedule Your Free SEO Consultation"
10. "Get a Free Strategy Call"
11. "Start Growing Locally or Nationally"

Eleven labels. Nine implied deliverables (session, consult, audit, call, review, analysis, demo, strategy report, consultation). All resolve to `/contact-us/`. This is one CTA displayed eleven times, described eleven different ways.

**Faults confirmed live:**

- **#10 links to `#`.** Dead button, positioned at the highest-intent micro-moment on the page (immediately after "Not sure if we're the right fit? Lets talk!").
- **#9 says "SEO"** on a social media page.
- The mid-page audit block — *"30 min Strategy call / In depth Audit / Growth Roadmap"* — is the **best CTA on the page** and the closest thing to the ICP's required offer. It is unnamed, appears once, and is buried between the process and the testimonials.

### Where does the page lose direction?

Three places:

1. **After the service tabs.** Eight pillars × six bullets each. Serotonin saturation with no adrenaline release — the reader has absorbed scope but has no differentiated next step.
2. **The comparison table.** Renders twice in the DOM with different rows in each version, including four rows imported from an eCommerce/fashion page. Direction is lost to visible error.
3. **The ending.** The page terminates on the FAQ accordion. No closing CTA block. The final impression is an unresolved question, not an invitation.

### Recommendations — Section 1

| # | Action | Priority |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Name the offer. **"Social Media Marketing Strategy Consultation"** becomes the single primary CTA sitewide on this page. | Critical |
| 1.2 | Collapse eleven labels into two: **Book a Social Media Marketing Strategy Consultation** (primary) / **Get a Free Social Media Audit** (secondary). | Critical |
| 1.3 | Attach a "what you walk away with" deliverables list to the primary CTA — a named artefact, per ICP §10 ("close to a hard requirement"). | Critical |
| 1.4 | Fix the `#` link. Rename the "SEO Consultation" button. | Critical |
| 1.5 | Promote the "30 min Strategy call / In depth Audit / Growth Roadmap" block to hero-adjacent, and name it. | High |
| 1.6 | Add a final CTA block after the FAQ. | High |

### Layer 1 — Dopamine (Attention + Desire): 6/10

**Working:** *"not just likes and follows"* is a genuinely sharp hook — it names the resentment Dani feels every Monday when she opens Looker Studio. The interactive goal selector is a strong desire device because it makes the visitor self-declare intent.

**Failing:**

- The hero claims *"an average 400% increase in audience reach and a 250% boost in lead generation."* **Reach is a vanity metric appearing in a hero that promises to move beyond vanity metrics.** The sentence cancels itself two lines after making its best point.
- For Dani specifically these numbers are worse than neutral — they are disqualifying. ICP §10: *"Guaranteed lead numbers read as amateurish and increase her suspicion."* ICP §6: she has *"a real, specific memory of paying for generic tiles."* Round unattributed percentages are the signature of the vendor that burned her.
- No specificity. No named client, no timeframe, no dollar value, no vertical.
- No consultation CTA above the fold.

**Fix:** one attributed, timeframed, verifiable result. Delete both percentages.

### Layer 2 — Oxytocin (Trust + Connection): 4/10 — weakest layer

**Working:** 20-logo client wall with recognisable Australian brands. Seven industry cards with contextual copy. Two case studies with specific, plausible tactics.

**Failing — five confirmed faults:**

1. **The string "Default Text" is printed above all five testimonials.** A live placeholder rendering to visitors. For a buyer whose primary screen is *"does their thinking hold up, or is it keyword-stuffed filler?"* (ICP §11), this is close to instant disqualification.
2. **Testimonials are unverifiable.** *"Emily R., Boutique Hotel Manager"* — no company, photo, logo or link. First-initial-only attribution reads as fabricated, and reads *worse* because 20 real client logos sit two screens above. The mismatch generates suspicion rather than trust.
3. **Case studies are anonymous but numerically precise.** *"Boutique Fitness Chain In Sydney"* + "220%", "55%", "300%". Precise figures from an unnamed source is the lowest-credibility configuration available. ICP §10: *"Named referees she can ring matter most of all."*
4. **No humans anywhere.** Zero team names, faces, or credentials. Dani's due-diligence order (ICP §11) explicitly includes *"the individual consultants' LinkedIn profiles, because she is assessing who she'd actually work with."* The page gives her nothing to assess.
5. **Copy-paste contamination breaks connection outright.** Confirmed live:
   - Services intro: *"Our SEO experts for the **fashion industry** assist brands that require strategies tailored to competitive retail and seasonal trends. We help you dominate style searches and turn clicks into buyers."*
   - Process Step 1: *"As part of our social media marketing services in **Wollongong**…"*
   - Comparison table: *"Review & trust optimisation for **fashion buyers"*, *"Clear ROI tied to sales & **add-to-cart**"*, *"No **eCommerce** tracking setup"*, *"Only send traffic reports"*
   - Section heading: *"**SEO** Expertise Across Australia"*
   - `<title>`, meta description, og:title and og:description all sell **Local SEO**
   - Image alt text references Local SEO, organic search traffic and Meta Ads dashboards across unrelated sections

**These are not cosmetic.** Dani is buying judgement. A page that says "fashion industry" in the intro to its social media services is evidence about how carefully her brand would be handled. This is the most damaging cluster of issues in the audit and it is also the cheapest to fix.

### Layer 3 — Serotonin (Authority + Certainty): 8/10 — strongest layer

**Working:**

- Seven named, sequential process steps, each with a described deliverable. Competitor benchmark: First Page runs 6 steps, OMG runs 3, SOUP runs 4 method blocks. **TrafficRadius has the most granular process in the set.** Genuine advantage.
- Eight service pillars each with a "We deliver:" list. Scope clarity exceeds every competitor page reviewed.
- **Social Media Analytics & Reporting** treated as its own pillar rather than a footnote — directly relevant to Dani's attribution problem.
- The three-way comparison device (Us / Typical Agencies / In-House) matches the strongest single section found in the competitor set (OMG's Typical Agency vs OMG vs In-House table).

**Failing:**

- **Zero credentials.** No platform partner badges, no review rating, no award, no years in business, no ad spend managed, no team size. Competitor benchmark is brutal here: First Page displays 9 partner badges, 14 awards, 4.9/5 from 500+ reviews, press logos and $3.8B+ client sales; OMG displays Clutch and Google awards, 4.9 from 630+ reviews, "14 years", "$200M+ managed media." TrafficRadius displays logos only. **In this set, TrafficRadius is the least credentialled page reviewed.**
- No named people. No case study attributed to a strategist.
- No professional services vertical anywhere — not in the 7 industry cards, not in the case studies, not in the FAQ. Dani screens hard for category specificity and *"retail and e-commerce examples actively disqualify a provider in her mind"* (ICP §10). The page's proof set is fitness, homeware, hotel, electrical, childcare, retail showroom. **Every single one disqualifies.**
- Eight pillars × six bullets creates a wall with no prioritisation.
- Comparison table duplicated in DOM with inconsistent rows.

### Layer 4 — Endorphin (Safety + Risk Reduction): 6/10

**Working:** The 12-question FAQ is the best-performing section on the page and competitive with the best in the set (First Page 11, OMG 12, SOUP 11). It handles time-to-results, content ownership, organic vs paid, in-house comparison, tracking and attribution, and closes with an explicit how-to-start answer. *"You'll approve key assets, then we handle scheduling"* is a good control signal.

### Layer 5 — Adrenaline (Urgency + Action): 5/10

Attention advantage in a finite local market is cumulative. That is legitimate urgency requiring no hype.

- **No closing CTA.** Page ends on an accordion.
- **No seasonal hook.** Dani's natural buying window is the post-lodgement period, roughly late November to February (ICP §9). Nothing on the page acknowledges that professional services firms have a planning season.

### Specific lines that misfire — and why

**1. Hero: "average 400% increase in audience reach and a 250% boost in lead generation"**
Reach is the metric she has stopped believing in. Unattributed round percentages match her scar tissue precisely (ICP §6: generic tiles, templated tax tips). Guaranteed-sounding lead numbers *"read as amateurish and increase her suspicion"* (ICP §10). **This is the single most disqualifying line on the page for this ICP.**
→ Replace with one named, timeframed professional services result, or a diagnostic statement.

**2. "Our SEO experts for the fashion industry assist brands… dominate style searches"**
Wrong service, wrong industry, in the intro to the main services block. For a buyer whose first screen is whether the thinking holds up, this is fatal on contact.
→ Replace with a professional-services-framed service introduction.

**3. Every case study and testimonial vertical**
Boutique fitness, homeware, boutique hotel, electrical contractor, fitness studio, childcare, retail showroom. Not one professional services firm. Under ICP §10 these do not merely fail to persuade — they *disqualify*.
→ Add at least one professional services case narrative. The ICP §14 illustrative scenarios provide four usable patterns (accounting firm operating-model fix; engineering consultancy pre-tender familiarity; law firm compliance framework; accounting firm where the recommendation was to deprioritise social).

**4. Industry cards: Trades, Hospitality, Education & Childcare, Fitness & Wellness, Local Retail, Building & Construction**
"Professional Services" exists as a card but its copy — *"Position your firm as an industry leader and attract high-value clients through thought leadership and targeted campaigns"* — is generic to the point of invisibility. **5. FAQ: "Is social media marketing useful for B2B companies too?"**
Answer is correct but pitched at a beginner: *"Yes, especially on LinkedIn… we build campaigns that position your team as thought leaders."* Dani knows this. Being explained things she already knows is described in the ICP as **"fatal."**
→ Rewrite at peer level: company page vs personal profiles and why the distribution model inverts, thought-leader ads, employee advocacy across 60 staff, dark-social attribution, self-reported attribution on enquiry forms.

**6. FAQ: "Will your agency create all the content, copy and graphics?" — "Yes."**
For most buyers this is reassurance. For Dani it is a **red flag**: an agency producing all content for a professional services firm is exactly how you get generic tiles with a stock photo of a calculator. Her firm's credibility lives in eight partners, not in a design template.
→ Expand the answer to describe a capture-and-amplify model: partner voice as the source material, structured interview as the input mechanism, engineered to minimal partner hours.

**7. Process Step 1 — "our social media marketing services in Wollongong"**
Wrong city, on a page that must establish Melbourne credibility. ICP §13, Opportunity 6: genuine local specificity decisively outperforms national scale claims with this buyer.
→ Delete. Replace with Melbourne / Victoria framing.

**8. "Schedule Your Free SEO Consultation" / "SEO Expertise Across Australia"**
Wrong service in a CTA and a section heading.

**9. Missing entirely: the root cause she half-suspects**
The ICP is unambiguous that the true root cause is the personal-brand-versus-firm-brand tension — *"professional services buyers hire people, not logos"* — and that naming it crisply is *"the moment trust forms"* (§13, Opportunity 3). The page never mentions it. Neither does any competitor page reviewed. **This is uncontested territory.**

**10. Missing entirely: partner-time efficiency as a headline promise**
ICP §13, Opportunity 4: *"Competitors sell content volume. Dani's binding constraint is partner hours… This should be front and centre, not buried in the process slide."* Currently absent at every level.

- **The comparison table's in-house column** — she is genuinely weighing in-house versus external.
