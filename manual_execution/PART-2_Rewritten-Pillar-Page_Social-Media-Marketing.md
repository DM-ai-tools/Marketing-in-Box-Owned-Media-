## [LOCKED HEADING] The Traffic Radius Effect

**H3:** Four ways social goes flat — and they don't look different from the dashboard

**Intro:**
Most of the businesses that come to us have already tried the obvious things. Better design. More consistency. A proper content calendar. Boosting the posts that did best. Maybe a paid test that got judged on cost per download and quietly shelved.

Each one produced a small, unsatisfying lift. That's not a content problem. That's a diagnosis problem — and there are four causes that produce almost identical symptoms.

**The four causes — as cards:**

**1. It's a channel-fit problem.**
You're spread across three or four platforms because that's what you're supposed to do, with no evidence behind any of them. Effort divides, nothing reaches critical mass, and every channel underperforms slightly rather than one channel working properly.

*The tell:* respectable impressions everywhere, meaningful response nowhere.

**2. It's a brand-architecture problem.**
Your credibility sits in your people — your partners, principals, senior practitioners, founders — and your social presence is a logo. In categories where buyers hire individuals rather than organisations, a company page is publishing into the wrong distribution model.

*The tell:* someone in your business posts something rough and unpolished from their phone and it outperforms six months of designed, brand-compliant, on-calendar content. If that has happened, this is very likely your primary cause. Most businesses read it as luck. It isn't — it's the mechanism telling you where attention actually lives.

**3. It's an operating-model problem.**
The strategy is sound. The content is fine. But production competes with billable work, approval takes nine days on a two-day news cycle, and the whole thing depends on two or three people who are busiest exactly when the plan needs them. It doesn't fail — it just quietly stops during your busy season and never restarts.

*The tell:* your best months are the months you had capacity, not the months your strategy changed.

**4. It's a measurement problem.**
Social may already be contributing and you can't see it. Last-click attribution in a category with a six-to-eighteen-month consideration cycle will always credit the final search. Dark social — the DM, the forwarded post, the conversation you never see — leaves no trace. If there's no self-reported attribution question on your enquiry form, "I've been seeing you around" never enters your data at all.

*The tell:* enquiries arriving warm, already familiar with you, and attributed to direct or branded search.

**Turn — H3:** Which one is yours?

Often it's two of them, weighted. Occasionally the honest answer is that social isn't your constraint at all — we've assessed businesses where the real bottleneck was enquiry response time, not visibility, and recommended they fix intake before spending another dollar on reach.

What we don't do is guess, and we don't sell you a retainer to find out. The **Social Media Marketing Strategy Consultation** exists to separate these causes with evidence, and to give you a written recommendation you can act on — with us, with your existing team, or with someone else.
