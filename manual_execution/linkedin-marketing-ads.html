The Company Page Trap

Why your LinkedIn company page can do everything "right" and still underperform

This is the single most common cause we see when a professional services firm's LinkedIn presence is flat — and it's a distribution problem, not a content problem. It shows up as four specific, connected symptoms.

1. Publishing from the company page first.

LinkedIn's algorithm surfaces individual profiles far more readily than company pages. A program built primarily around the company account is publishing into the channel LinkedIn itself favours least.

The tell: decent impressions on the company page, almost no comments, DMs, or "saw your post" conversations.

2. No individual voice activated.

Your credibility sits with your partners, principals and senior practitioners — but your LinkedIn presence is a logo. Someone posts something rough and unscripted from their own profile, and it quietly outperforms months of designed company content.

The tell: if that's already happened once at your firm, this is very likely your primary cause.

3. No Thought Leader Ads.

Running a named person's organic content as paid, to a tightly defined firmographic audience, is the format that actually works in this category — and it's a different exercise from gating a whitepaper and judging it on cost per download.

The tell: paid social exists, but it's all sponsored content from the company page.

4. Employee advocacy left on the table.

A 60-person firm has 60 relevant networks. Almost nobody activates them deliberately, and it's usually the largest untapped distribution asset a firm already owns.

The tell: reach that never seems to move, despite a team full of people with relevant connections.
