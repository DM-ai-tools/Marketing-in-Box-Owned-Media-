## PART 2 — WEBINAR STRATEGY & ARCHITECTURE

*(Part 1 is intentionally absent in this version — see note above.)*

### 2A. Strategic Rationale
Without competitive intelligence to calibrate against, the default, well-supported approach for a problem-aware B2B audience is a "signs/mistakes" format: it's a proven, familiar webinar shape, it's easy to promote (each sign is its own teaser hook), and it maps cleanly onto this ICP's documented pains and objections. The risk this approach carries — one this version cannot see or correct for, precisely because it skipped competitor research — is that "signs your strategy is failing" is a common format in this category; there is no way to know from this reasoning alone whether it overlaps with something a competitor is already doing well.

### 2B. Webinar Title & Subtitle
**Title:** *5 Signs Your Social Media Strategy Needs a Rethink (And What To Do About Each One)*
**Subtitle:** *A practical session for Melbourne professional services firms who've invested in social media but aren't seeing it convert.*

### 2C. Big Promise
By the end of this session, you'll be able to identify which of five common warning signs applies to your firm's social media, and know the single next action for each one.

**[Module 1: Sign 1 — Flat Engagement Despite Consistent Posting]**
Duration: 6 min | CRO Layer: Serotonin
Slide Cue: Flat-line graph illustration.

Speaker Script:
"The first sign is the most common one: you've been posting consistently, the content is genuinely fine, and engagement has flatlined anyway. Consistency without a distribution strategy plateaus — posting regularly gets you a baseline, but it doesn't compound unless something else is driving reach alongside it. If this is you, the fix isn't posting more often; it's finding the actual distribution lever, whether that's a specific platform, a specific voice, or paid amplification of what's already working."

Transition Line: "Sign two is specific to professional services, and it catches a lot of firms out."

---

**[Module 2: Sign 2 — Company Voice, No Individual Voice]**
Duration: 7 min | CRO Layer: Oxytocin
Slide Cue: Company logo post vs. named individual post (illustrative).

Speaker Script:
"The second sign: everything comes from the company page, and nothing comes from a named person. In professional services, buyers hire people, not organisations — so a content strategy that's 100% company voice is missing the mechanism that actually builds trust in this category. The fix is building a simple, low-effort way for your senior people to show up as themselves."

Transition Line: "Sign three has nothing to do with strategy — it's about capacity."

---

**[Module 3: Sign 3 — Content Bottlenecked by Busy People]**
Duration: 7 min | CRO Layer: Serotonin
Slide Cue: Calendar graphic showing gaps clustered around "busy periods."

Speaker Script:
"Sign three: your best content comes from your busiest people, which means it only happens when they happen to have time. That's not a discipline problem — it's an operating-model problem. The fix is a process that captures their expertise quickly (a short conversation, a quick voice note) rather than asking them to write and design content themselves."

Transition Line: "Sign four is about whether you can actually prove any of this is working."

---

**[Module 4: Sign 4 — Can't Prove It's Working]**
Duration: 6 min | CRO Layer: Endorphin
Slide Cue: Simple attribution funnel graphic.

Speaker Script:
"Sign four: you genuinely don't know if social is contributing to the business, because your attribution is built around last-click search data. If someone reads your content and later just searches your name, social gets zero credit for the actual work it did. If this is your situation, the fix is measurement, not content."

Transition Line: "Sign five is the quiet one — nobody officially owns it."

---

**[Module 5: Sign 5 — No Clear Owner or Process]**
Duration: 6 min | CRO Layer: Serotonin
Slide Cue: Org chart with a question mark where "social media owner" should sit.

Speaker Script:
"The fifth sign: nobody at the firm can clearly say who's responsible for social, or what the process actually is week to week. Ad hoc effort, however good the content is individually, doesn't compound without a process and an accountable owner behind it."

Transition Line: "Let's do a quick self-check against all five."

**[Q&A Framing — 5 Pre-Seeded Questions]**

1. **"What if we recognise three or four of these signs at once?"** That's common — we help prioritise which to fix first based on impact and effort.
2. **"Does this apply if we're not using paid ads at all?"** Yes — all five signs are about organic strategy and operations, not spend.
3. **"How is the consultation different from just doing this checklist ourselves?"** We bring outside evidence and pattern-matching from other firms — useful when you're too close to your own situation to be objective.
4. **"How much of our time does the consultation take?"** Stated in hours upfront — typically one 60-minute session to start.
5. **"Is this a sales call in disguise?"** No — it's a scoped, written deliverable you keep regardless of what you decide afterward.

---

## WHAT'S ACTUALLY DIFFERENT BETWEEN THE TWO OUTPUTS

- **Angle:** Output 1's "diagnose one of four causes" framing directly exploits a gap no competitor currently occupies (verified against 6 real competitor webinar programs). Output 2's "5 warning signs" framing is a conventional, well-supported webinar format chosen from general best practice — it has no way of knowing whether a competitor is already running something similar, because it deliberately didn't check.
- **Credibility strategy:** Output 1 explicitly references what competitors do well (founder-led authority, scale/credibility proof) and improves on it; Output 2's credibility approach is generic best-practice ("brief, proof-led intro") with no competitive calibration.
- **Objection handling:** Output 1's Q&A questions were selected partly because competitor content visibly fails to address them (e.g. "is this generic advice with our logo on it?"); Output 2's Q&A questions are generated from the ICP profile alone, without that competitive lens.
- **Risk:** Output 2 cannot rule out unintentionally overlapping with what a competitor already does — that's the direct cost of skipping Step 2, made explicit here rather than hidden.
