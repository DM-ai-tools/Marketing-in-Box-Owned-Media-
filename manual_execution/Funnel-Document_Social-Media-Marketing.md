# PART 1 — FUNNEL STRATEGY & ARCHITECTURE

## 1A. Strategic Rationale

Dani is not shopping. She is diagnosing. The CRO audit and the ICP document both establish that she is problem-aware and stuck at "I don't know what's wrong," not at "I'm comparing vendors." That fact governs every decision in this funnel.

**Why a Free Social Media Audit as the primary lead magnet:**
The pillar page already positions "Get a Free Social Media Audit" as the secondary CTA beneath the named **Social Media Marketing Strategy Consultation**. This funnel exists to give that secondary CTA somewhere real to go — a proper landing page, a qualifying form, a delivery mechanism and a follow-up sequence — rather than dumping it into the same generic contact form as everything else. It also matches her decision criteria directly: ICP §10 states she needs *"a defined, bounded, low-risk first step"* and *"a tangible artefact she can take to the partner group."* An audit is lower commitment than booking a 30-minute call cold, and it produces exactly the kind of written artefact she needs.

**Why a Community as the secondary lead magnet:**
ICP §11 is explicit: *"Peer conversation is her highest-trust channel… A single credible peer recommendation shortcuts most of her evaluation."* She is already active in a professional services marketing peer group (Slack/WhatsApp) and reads LinkedIn passively for months before ever engaging. A branded community — not a form, not a commitment, just a place to lurk and compare notes with peers — matches her actual research behaviour better than any gated PDF would. It is also a zero-friction entry point for the visitor who isn't ready to hand over business details for an audit yet, which the competitor research (Part 2 below) confirms nobody else in this category offers.

**The competitive gap this exploits:**
Every competitor funnel reviewed (WebBuzz, BubbleGum, SOUP) leads with a generic "free consultation" or "free assessment" — a phone-call-first mechanic with no diagnostic artefact and no low-commitment entry rung beneath it. None of them names or brands their audit. None of them offers a community. This funnel does both, and it sequences them correctly: community for the not-yet-ready visitor, audit for the ready-to-engage visitor, consultation as the paid-attention conversion at the end.

**Connection to the pillar page:**
This funnel does not introduce new claims, new proof, or new positioning. It reuses the pillar page's exact language — the four causes (channel-fit, brand-architecture, operating-model, measurement), the "flat line in an otherwise healthy funnel" framing, the no-lock-in and ownership commitments, and the same two CTA labels (**Book a Social Media Marketing Strategy Consultation** / **Get a Free Social Media Audit**). Nothing here contradicts anything already published.

# PART 2 — COMPETITOR FUNNEL ANALYSIS

## 2A. Funnel Type Distribution

Ten Australian agencies were identified as funnel-service competitors (`funnel_competitors_australia.xlsx`). Four were reviewed in depth (WebBuzz, BubbleGum Marketing, Thrive Digital, SOUP Agency); Thrive Digital's page failed to load usable content (JS-rendered shell) and is excluded from findings below.

| Funnel type | Prevalence in this set |
|---|---|
| **Free consultation / strategy session as the sole front door** | Dominant. WebBuzz ("FREE Assessment"), BubbleGum ("FREE Strategy Session, valued at $497" + a separate "15-Minute Consultation"), SOUP ("Let's Talk Growth" / "Schedule a Call") all lead with a call-booking mechanic. |
| **Gated content asset (guide/report)** | Present but secondary and under-invested. WebBuzz has a white paper and a masterclass buried in global nav, not featured on the funnel page itself. SOUP's gated asset is a **"Social Media Digital Channel 2023 Report"** — visibly stale, dated three years behind the page's own timestamp. |
| **Interactive diagnostic (quiz, calculator, scorecard)** | **Absent across the entire reviewed set.** No competitor offers a self-service tool that produces an instant, personalised result. |
| **Community / membership as a lead mechanic** | **Absent across the entire reviewed set.** BubbleGum mentions membership sites and affiliate programs only as services they *build for clients* — not something they run themselves to capture leads. |
| **Named, branded audit** | **Absent.** WebBuzz's "Free Assessment" is the closest analogue, but it is a repeated button label, not a built-out diagnostic with a described input/output and its own landing page. |

## 2B. Lead Magnet Landscape

**What's present:** phone-call-first consultations (all three), one dollar-anchored offer ($497 value, BubbleGum), one dated PDF report (SOUP).

**What's conspicuously absent:**
- No named, branded audit product anywhere in the set
- No interactive tool of any kind (quiz, calculator, scorecard, "grade your funnel in 60 seconds")
- No community or peer-group mechanic
- No visible post-submission nurture sequence — none of the three reviewed sites shows "check your inbox" language or any evidence of a structured follow-up beyond the booking calendar

## 2C. Competitive Value Ladder (typical, as observed in this market)

```
Free (generic call booking) → Front-end (strategy/discovery engagement, price opaque)
  → Core (ongoing retainer, price opaque) → High-ticket (multi-channel/enterprise
  retainer) → Retention (no visible loyalty or expansion mechanic observed)
```

The ladder in this category is short and undifferentiated at the entry rung: nearly every competitor's first rung is "book a call," which asks for the highest-commitment action a cold visitor can take before they've received any value or built any trust. There is no lower rung beneath it.

## 2D. Our Differentiation

| Dimension | Competitor pattern | TrafficRadius funnel |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead magnet format** | Generic "free assessment/consultation" (phone call, no artefact) or a stale, ungated-feeling report | A **named, structured audit** with a described process and a written deliverable, positioned exactly like the pillar page's diagnostic framing (four causes, not generic tips) |
| **ICP specificity** | All three competitors write for a generic SMB owner; none references professional services, partner time, compliance review, or long consideration cycles | Every page in this funnel is written for Dani specifically — her exact symptoms, her exact objections, her exact language |
| **Value delivery speed** | Call-booking requires scheduling and waiting for an available slot with no interim value | The audit delivers a written artefact within a stated window (2–3 business days) with no call required to receive it |
| **Friction level** | Single friction point: book a call (high commitment for a cold, problem-aware visitor) | Two friction levels offered: near-zero (join the Community) or moderate (submit the audit request) — visitor self-selects based on readiness |
| **Trust mechanism** | Dollar-anchoring ($497 value) is the only credibility device found, and it's a claimed value rather than demonstrated expertise | The audit process itself demonstrates diagnostic credibility before any sales conversation — exactly the trust mechanism ICP §10 identifies as decisive: *"The provider who demonstrates in the first fifteen minutes that they have seen her exact pattern before… wins before commercials are discussed."* Here, that demonstration happens in the audit itself, before any conversation at all. |
