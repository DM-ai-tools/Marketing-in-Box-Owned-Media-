01
⚑ Critical
No Creative Testing Strategy — They'll Run One Ad and Hope
🚩
What to look for

A serious Meta Ads agency runs systematic creative testing — A/B testing headlines, images, hooks, and formats on a scheduled cadence. If the proposal doesn't mention creative refresh cycles, split testing methodology, or how they'll identify winning ad variants, your budget will be spent on a single creative until it burns out.

  • ✓No mention of A/B testing or creative split testing
  • ✓No creative refresh schedule or fatigue management plan
  • ✓Can't explain how they identify a winning ad variant
  • ✓Only plans to run one ad set at launch with no iteration plan
Warning Signals
"We'll create your ads and monitor them"
No mention of creative fatigue or frequency management
Talks about creative quality but not creative volume
No content calendar or refresh cadence included
The Real Risk

Without creative testing, campaigns plateau within 4–6 weeks as audiences see the same ads repeatedly. You'll pay for frequency, not results.

Ask them: "How many creative variants will you test monthly, and what's your process for identifying winners?"
Mark as spotted →
02
⚑ Critical
One-Size-Fits-All Audiences — No Cold, Warm, or Retargeting Strategy
🚩
What to look for

Effective Meta Ads require a three-layer audience architecture: cold prospecting (reaching new audiences), warm nurturing (retargeting website visitors and video viewers), and hot retargeting (reaching people who nearly converted). A proposal that treats all audiences the same is structurally incapable of building a profitable funnel.

  • ✓No distinction between cold, warm, and retargeting audiences
  • ✓No mention of custom audiences, lookalikes, or pixel-based retargeting
  • ✓Can't explain their audience warm-up or nurture sequence
  • ✓No budget split between prospecting and retargeting mentioned
"We'll target people interested in your category"
No lookalike audience strategy discussed
Mentions retargeting but only as a footnote
No audience testing or exclusion strategy
The Real Risk

Running cold ads to all audiences simultaneously burns budget on people who need to be nurtured, not sold to. You'll get impressions, not conversions.

Ask them: "Walk me through the three audience layers you'll build for our account and how budget will be allocated across them."
Mark as spotted →
03
▲ High
No Clear Attribution Model — They Can't Explain How They Track Conversions
🚩
What to look for

If an agency can't explain exactly how they'll track conversions — which events fire, which attribution window they use, how they reconcile Meta's data with your CRM or website analytics — they will either over-report results or have no idea whether their campaigns are working. Both outcomes cost you money.

  • ✓No mention of Meta Pixel setup or Conversions API
  • ✓Can't explain what attribution window (7-day click, 1-day view) they use
  • ✓No plan to reconcile Meta-reported leads with actual CRM entries
  • ✓Doesn't mention Google Analytics cross-referencing
"We use Meta's built-in reporting" — no mention of pixel
Can't explain 1-day view vs 7-day click attribution
Promises ROAS but can't explain how it's calculated
No GA4 or third-party analytics integration plan
The Real Risk

Without proper attribution, Meta will claim credit for sales that happened organically — inflating your reported ROAS and hiding the real picture. You'll think the campaign is working when it may not be.

Ask them: "What attribution window do you use, and how will you verify Meta-reported conversions against our actual sales records?"
Mark as spotted →
04
▲ High
Vanity Metric Reporting — Reach, Impressions, and Likes Instead of ROAS and CPL
🚩
What to look for

If the proposal's reporting section highlights reach, impressions, likes, or followers as primary success indicators, the agency is optimising for metrics that feel impressive but don't grow your revenue. You cannot pay your bills with impressions. Demand to know exactly which business outcomes will appear on every report.

  • ✓Reporting sample shows reach/impressions as headline metrics
  • ✓No Cost Per Lead (CPL) or ROAS column in sample report
  • ✓Monthly report doesn't include revenue or conversion data
  • ✓Success is defined by engagement rate rather than lead quality
"Your reach increased by 42%" — but leads didn't
Sample report shows impressions as the #1 KPI
Mentions "brand awareness" as the campaign goal
Can't show you a sample ROAS or CPL report
The Real Risk

Agencies that report vanity metrics know they're difficult to connect to revenue — which means under-performing campaigns can hide indefinitely behind impressive-looking numbers. If it doesn't connect to revenue, it doesn't matter.

Ask them: "Can you show me a sample monthly report? I want to see Cost Per Lead, ROAS, and total conversions as the headline metrics."
Mark as spotted →
05
▲ High
No Dedicated Account Manager — You'll Be Passed Around or Left Waiting
🚩
What to look for

The proposal is vague about who specifically will manage your account. You're dealing with a sales team now — but who runs your campaigns day to day? If there's no named account manager, guaranteed response time, or escalation path in the proposal, you risk being deprioritised once the contract is signed.

  • ✓Proposal doesn't name who will manage your specific account
  • ✓No guaranteed response time for queries or issues stated
  • ✓No regular strategy call or check-in schedule offered
  • ✓High client-to-manager ratio suggested or implied
"Our team will handle your account" — no specific name
No SLA (service level agreement) for response times
Monthly call only offered on premium tier
Can't tell you how many clients each manager handles
The Real Risk

Without a dedicated manager, your account becomes a number in a queue. Campaign issues go unnoticed for days. Opportunities are missed. You're paying for management that doesn't happen.

Ask them: "Who is my dedicated account manager, how many clients do they manage, and what's your guaranteed response time for urgent issues?"
Mark as spotted →
06
▲ High
No Discovery Process — They'll Run Ads Without Understanding Your Business
🚩
What to look for

A serious agency doesn't send a proposal before they understand your business. Before quoting, they should have asked about your average customer value, your margins, your sales process, your existing audience, and your competitive landscape. If the proposal looks like it could have been sent to any business in your category, it probably was.

  • ✓Proposal feels generic — could apply to any business in your niche
  • ✓Never asked about your average customer lifetime value or margins
  • ✓No mention of your existing customer data, pixel history, or audiences
  • ✓Strategy section lacks any reference to your specific competitive market
Generic proposal with your logo pasted in
Never asked about your sales process or conversion rate
Discovery call was 20 minutes with no deep questions
No mention of your customer avatar or buying journey
The Real Risk

An agency that doesn't understand your business before launch will learn on your budget. The first 60–90 days will be expensive experiments rather than informed strategy. You pay for their education.

Ask them: "Before we start, can we walk through our customer lifecycle, average order value, and current funnel — so you can build a strategy around our actual numbers?"

Quick Reference
Three Types of Proposals — Know Which One You're Looking At

Not every proposal with a flaw is a deal-breaker. Here's how to read what you're seeing.

⛔
Walk Away Immediately
  • No specific performance targets or KPIs of any kind
  • Refuses to show sample reports or past client results
  • No clear attribution or conversion tracking plan included
  • You cannot identify who will manage your account
⚠️
Negotiate Before Signing
  • Targets are vague but they clarify when pushed
  • Reporting is light but they can customise on request
  • No creative testing mentioned but open to discussing it
  • Audience strategy is basic but they have a framework
✅
What a Strong Proposal Looks Like
  • Specific CPL targets and ROAS benchmarks for your industry
  • Named account manager with defined response times
  • Three-layer audience strategy (cold/warm/hot) explained clearly
  • Monthly creative testing cadence with fatigue management plan
