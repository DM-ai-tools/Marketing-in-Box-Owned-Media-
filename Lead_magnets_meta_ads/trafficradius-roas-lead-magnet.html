01 Step
Critical — Check This First

Verify Your Pixel & Conversion Tracking

~3 min
Audit Checklist
  • Open Meta Events Manager and verify your pixel shows "Active" status with data in the last 24 hours
  • Use the Meta Pixel Helper browser extension to confirm your key conversion event (Purchase, Lead, or Submit Application) is firing correctly on the thank-you/confirmation page
  • Check your Aggregated Event Measurement settings — confirm your highest-priority conversion event is ranked first in your pixel's event configuration
  • Verify your attribution window hasn't changed — compare the window used this month against last month's reporting period (7-day click vs 1-day click changes ROAS significantly)
Warning Signals
  • ! Red flag: Events Manager shows "No recent activity" or pixel status is "Inactive" — your ROAS drop may be entirely a measurement failure, not a real performance issue
  • ~ Watch for: A sudden shift in conversion volume that coincides with a website update, theme change, or checkout flow modification — these commonly break pixel events silently
  • ✓ Healthy signal: Pixel firing, correct events ranked, attribution window unchanged — proceed to Step 2 with confidence that your data is reliable
Key Finding
A misconfigured pixel or broken conversion event is the single most common cause of a "false" ROAS drop. Rule this out before touching anything else in your account.
If issues found
Reinstall the pixel via your CMS (Shopify, WordPress, Webflow) or tag manager. Re-verify using Test Events in Events Manager before re-enabling any paused campaigns. Do not adjust campaigns until tracking is confirmed accurate.
02 Step
High Priority

Diagnose Audience Saturation & Fatigue

~4 min
Audit Checklist
  • Check frequency for each active ad set over the last 7 days — flag any ad set with frequency above 3.5 for a cold audience or above 7 for a warm retargeting audience
  • Review CPM trend week-over-week in the Ads Manager breakdowns — a rising CPM with stable or falling CTR confirms your audience is resisting the ad, not just the market
  • Check your audience size — audiences below 100,000 people saturate quickly when spend is maintained; lookalike audiences may need broadening or refreshing
  • Review your Quality Ranking and Engagement Rate Ranking in the Ads Manager columns — "Below Average" on either metric signals the audience has seen enough of this creative
Warning Signals
  • ! Red flag: Frequency above 4.0 on a cold audience with a CPM that has risen more than 25% over 14 days — your audience is exhausted and the algorithm is working harder for diminishing results
  • ~ Watch for: Audience size shrinking as you add exclusion lists without adding fresh audiences — your reachable pool may have quietly contracted
  • ✓ Healthy signal: Frequency below 2.5, stable CPM, audience size above 500k — audience health is not your primary ROAS issue; advance to Step 3
Key Finding
Audience saturation accounts for approximately 38% of ROAS declines in accounts spending over $5,000/month. The fix — expanding your audiences — often restores ROAS within 5–7 days.
If issues found
Launch a 2–4% lookalike audience to expand reach. Add an Advantage+ broad targeting ad set alongside your existing targeted sets. Exclude people who converted in the last 30 days to keep your pool fresh.
03 Step
High Priority

Audit Creative Performance & Fatigue Signals

~3 min
Audit Checklist
  • Check link CTR on your active ads — a CTR below 0.9% on cold audiences or below 1.8% on warm audiences indicates the creative is no longer compelling attention
  • For video ads, review your 3-second video view rate (hook rate) — a hook rate below 20% means your opening frame is failing to stop the scroll
  • Identify how long your current best-performing ads have been active — any ad running the same creative for more than 4–6 weeks without a fresh challenger is in the danger zone
  • Check your ad-level Quality Ranking — "Below Average (bottom 35% of ads)" signals Meta's algorithm has assessed your creative as significantly less engaging than competing ads in the same auction
Warning Signals
  • ! Red flag: CTR has dropped more than 30% from its peak while impressions remain stable — classic creative fatigue. The algorithm is still serving the ad, but your audience has stopped engaging with it
  • ~ Watch for: Strong hook rate but low conversion rate — the creative is stopping the scroll but the message isn't converting interest into action, suggesting a message-offer mismatch
  • ✓ Healthy signal: CTR stable or above benchmark, hook rate above 25%, creative launched within the last 3 weeks — creative is not your primary issue; advance to Step 4
Key Finding
Creative fatigue is the most common driver of gradual ROAS decline in mature accounts. Unlike audience saturation, it affects accounts of all sizes and budget levels equally.
If issues found
Launch two new creative variants immediately: one with a new hook angle and one with a new format (e.g. switch static to video or carousel). Use Dynamic Creative Testing to let Meta identify the winner within 7–10 days.
04 Step
Medium Priority

Review Landing Page & Offer Alignment

~3 min
Audit Checklist
  • Pull your landing page conversion rate for the last 30 days and compare it to the prior 30-day period — a drop of more than 15% is significant and points to a below-the-click problem
  • Test your landing page load speed on mobile using Google PageSpeed Insights — a load time above 3 seconds on mobile can reduce conversion rates by up to 40%
  • Compare the headline and primary offer on your landing page with the ad that drove the click — message match must be nearly identical or conversion rates will suffer
  • Check if your pricing, offer, or product range changed during the period of ROAS decline — an offer change that wasn't reflected in the ad copy creates expectation mismatch and tanks conversions
Warning Signals
  • ! Red flag: Click volume is healthy but lead/purchase volume has dropped sharply — the problem is occurring after the click, on your landing page or checkout flow, not in the ad
  • ~ Watch for: A recent CMS update, plugin change, or third-party integration that may have introduced a form error or page layout issue — these break conversion flows silently and often go unnoticed for weeks
  • ✓ Healthy signal: Landing page conversion rate stable, page speed under 2.5 seconds, message match strong — your below-the-click funnel is not the issue; advance to Step 5
Key Finding
Landing page issues often present as an "ads problem" because the click data looks healthy. ROAS drops caused by landing page failures are invisible in Ads Manager — they require checking your analytics platform directly.
If issues found
Fix page speed first (compress images, reduce scripts). Align your landing page headline directly to your ad headline. Test your form or checkout process end-to-end on a mobile device to catch any functional failures.
05 Step
Medium Priority

Analyse Budget, Bidding & Campaign Structure

~3 min
Audit Checklist
  • Review whether your daily or campaign budget changed (up or down) by more than 20% in the period before your ROAS dropped — large budget changes force campaigns back into the learning phase
  • Check your campaign's "Learning" or "Learning Limited" status in Ads Manager — campaigns in the learning phase typically underperform by 20–40% compared to stable delivery
  • Identify any bid strategy changes made in the last 30 days — switching from Lowest Cost to Cost Cap, or between manual and automated bidding, resets the algorithm's optimisation model
  • Count the number of significant edits made to active campaigns in the last 30 days (ad set pausing, duplicating, budget changes, audience edits) — more than 3–4 significant edits per week pushes campaigns back into learning repeatedly
Warning Signals
  • ! Red flag: Multiple campaigns showing "Learning Limited" status — this means Meta cannot get enough conversion events (minimum 50 per week per ad set) to optimise effectively, and ROAS will remain suppressed
  • ~ Watch for: Excessive campaign edits — well-intentioned optimisations made too frequently can create a "never leaves learning" situation where Meta's algorithm never stabilises
  • ✓ Healthy signal: Campaigns in "Active" status, budget changes minimal, bid strategy stable for 30+ days, less than 2 significant structural edits per week
Key Finding
Over-management is a real cause of ROAS decline. The Meta algorithm needs stability and consistent conversion volume to optimise. Sometimes the best action is to stop making changes and give the algorithm time to learn.
If issues found
If in learning phase: avoid all structural edits for 7 days and allow at least 50 conversions per ad set per week to exit learning. Consolidate ad sets if budget is spread too thin. Consider switching to Advantage+ Shopping Campaigns if currently running manual structures.

Recovery Actions

ROAS Recovery: What to Fix First

Based on the most common issue combinations identified in this diagnostic, here are the three most effective recovery actions — ranked by time-to-impact.

Fix Your Tracking (Day 1)

Immediate — 0–24 hours
  • Open Events Manager and check pixel status
  • Use Pixel Helper to test conversion event firing
  • Use Test Events tool to confirm live data
  • Review Aggregated Event Measurement priority ranking

Refresh Creative (Days 2–4)

Short-term — 2–7 days to impact
  • Produce two new creative variants with fresh hooks
  • Launch via Dynamic Creative Testing within existing ad sets
  • Do not pause winning creatives — add challengers alongside them
  • Review performance at 7 days and archive underperformers

Expand Audiences (Days 3–7)

Medium-term — 5–14 days to impact
  • Launch a 2–4% lookalike from your best customer list
  • Add an Advantage+ broad targeting ad set at 20% of budget
  • Review and update exclusion lists (remove converters older than 90 days)
  • Allow 10–14 days before evaluating new audience performance
