Question One

"What does your contract look like — and what exactly happens if we want to leave?"

Why this question matters most. Lock-in is the single biggest source of bad agency outcomes for small and medium businesses. Many agencies require 6–12 month minimum terms, hold your setup fees as a "deposit" against early exit, or retain ownership of your Google Ads account — meaning if you leave, you lose your campaign history, conversion data, and audience lists. You're starting from scratch and paying for the privilege.
🚩 Red Flags
  • Minimum 6–12 month contract with penalties
  • Setup fee withheld if you exit early
  • Account created under the agency's Google MCC
  • "We need time to optimise — 90 days minimum"
  • Vague answer about what "leaving" involves
✅ Green Flags
  • Month-to-month with 30 days' notice
  • Account is in your name, full admin access
  • No exit penalties, no retained fees
  • Clear written process for account handover
  • Confident to let the results speak each month
How Traffic Radius Answers This

Month-to-month from day one. Your Google Ads account is created in your name with you as owner — we operate as a manager, never as the owner. If you leave for any reason, you keep your account, your campaign history, your conversion data, and your audience lists. We ask for 30 days' notice as a courtesy so we can document and hand over cleanly. We are confident enough in our results to not need a contract to keep your business.

Question Two

"Who owns my Google Ads account — and can I log in at any time?"

Why account ownership is a bigger deal than it sounds. This is the question most business owners don't think to ask until it's too late. An agency that creates your campaign inside their own Google Manager Account (MCC) effectively holds your entire campaign history hostage. Your conversion tracking, bid history, Quality Scores, remarketing audiences — all of it belongs to them if it lives in their account. Switching agencies doesn't just mean a new relationship; it means starting your Google Ads presence from zero.
🚩 Red Flags
  • "We'll set everything up for you" (no mention of ownership)
  • Hesitation when asked about admin access
  • "You won't need to log in — that's what we're for"
  • Account is under the agency's MCC with you as a viewer only
  • Proprietary dashboard that replaces native Google Ads access
✅ Green Flags
  • Account created in your Google account — you are the owner
  • Agency added as a manager with appropriate access level
  • You can see every campaign, every keyword, every bid
  • No proprietary lock-in layer between you and the platform
  • Comfortable walking you through the account on screen
How Traffic Radius Answers This

Your account, always. We link to your Google Ads account as a manager — we never own it. You have permanent admin access and can see every campaign, every keyword, every negative keyword, every bid adjustment, every conversion at any time. We actively encourage clients to check in. An agency that discourages you from looking at your own account is an agency with something to hide.

Question Three

"What does your reporting actually look like — can I see a real sample right now?"

Why this separates competent agencies from impressive-looking ones. Most agency reports are designed to look impressive, not to be useful. Dense PDFs with dozens of charts — clicks, impressions, Quality Score trends — that never tell you what you actually need to know: how much did each won job cost? How many qualified leads did this generate? What happened this week that's actionable? The business owners who are most frustrated with agencies are almost always the ones who got impressive-looking reports they couldn't act on.
🚩 Red Flags
  • Monthly-only reporting ("that's industry standard")
  • Clicks and impressions without conversion data
  • No cost-per-lead or cost-per-won-job metric
  • "We'll walk you through it on a monthly call"
  • Report only readable on desktop in PDF format
✅ Green Flags
  • Weekly updates as a minimum
  • One-page format readable on mobile
  • Cost per lead and cost per qualified enquiry reported
  • Plain English — no jargon, no vanity metrics
  • Willing to show you a real (anonymised) sample today
How Traffic Radius Answers This

We built our reporting around one question: can a business owner read this on their phone in 90 seconds and know exactly how their ads are performing? Weekly. One page. Spend, leads, cost per lead, conversion rate — in plain English, no jargon. We'll show you a real anonymised sample on any discovery call. If an agency won't show you their actual report format before you sign, that is the answer.

Question Four

"What experience do you have with businesses at my average job value and sales cycle?"

Why your job value and sales cycle change everything about how campaigns should be run. Google Ads for a $40 e-commerce purchase looks nothing like Google Ads for a $6,000 electrical installation or a $50,000 commercial build. The bidding logic, the keyword strategy, the landing page goal, the conversion measurement, the acceptable cost-per-lead — all different. An agency that mostly manages retail accounts and "also does" trade or professional services is likely applying a retail framework to a completely different problem. Ask for specific examples, not general claims.
🚩 Red Flags
  • "We work across all industries" with no specific examples
  • Can't tell you a typical CPL for your sector in Australia
  • Focused on clicks and click-through rate as primary metrics
  • Case studies are all e-commerce or retail
  • No mention of close rates or cost-per-won-job
✅ Green Flags
  • Has named clients or anonymised case studies in your sector
  • Knows industry benchmark CPL ranges off the top of their head
  • Understands the difference between a lead and a won job
  • Talks about close rate and sales cycle, not just clicks
  • Can explain how bidding strategy differs for high-value, low-volume
How Traffic Radius Answers This

We specialise in trades, construction, and professional service businesses — the exact businesses where Google Ads is most often set up wrong because it was configured using retail logic. We know that a commercial electrician in Melbourne typically pays $90–$120 per lead. We know that a 20% close rate on directory leads is below benchmark. We know that a $5,000 average job changes the acceptable CPL completely. We have the case studies and the numbers. Ask us on a call.

Question Five

"What exactly happens in the first 30 days — and how do you handle negative keywords from day one?"

Why the first 30 days decide whether the next 12 months are worth having. The most avoidable Google Ads waste happens in the first month: broad match keywords eating budget on irrelevant searches, no negative keyword list, campaigns running to generic landing pages, conversion tracking firing on the wrong event. A competent agency has a documented, structured onboarding process. They should be able to tell you exactly what happens on Day 1, Week 1, and Week 4 before they've seen your account. Vagueness here is a very bad sign.
🚩 Red Flags
  • "We run a test period and optimise as we go"
  • No mention of negative keywords in the first conversation
  • Vague about what "setup" includes
  • No documented 30-day onboarding plan
  • "It takes 90 days to see real results" with no explanation of why
✅ Green Flags
  • Can hand you a written 30-day onboarding roadmap
  • Mentions negative keywords proactively before you ask
  • Conversion tracking audit before a single dollar is spent
  • Specific Week 1 deliverables (structure, keywords, tracking)
  • Clear explanation of what "learning period" means and how long
How Traffic Radius Answers This

Day 1: we audit your existing conversion tracking (or set it up from scratch). Week 1: campaign structure, keyword research, and a 200+ negative keyword list built before a single ad goes live. Week 2: campaigns launch with exact and phrase match only — broad match comes later, once we have data. Week 4: first optimisation review with you. We give every new client a written 30-day plan before they sign. If an agency can't tell you what happens in the first four weeks, they don't have a process — they have a habit.
