Why Google Ads isn't just for e-commerce — and never really was

If you've had the quiet suspicion that Google Ads is mostly for online stores selling $40 products, you're not alone — and you're not entirely wrong that most of the case studies are written for that audience. But the platform itself has never been about transactions. It's about intent.

Every day in Australia, thousands of people search for "commercial electrician Melbourne," "structural engineer for home extension," "accountant for small business near me." These aren't window shoppers. These aren't people comparing products on a whim. These are people who have already decided they need something — and are choosing who to hire.

That's a completely different buying behaviour from retail. And it's precisely the behaviour Google Search Ads were designed to capture.

⚠️
The belief worth questioning "Google Ads works when someone can click 'buy' — my customers take weeks to decide and require a phone call and a quote. The model doesn't fit."
✅
What the data actually shows The longer and more considered the purchase, the more a business owner has been trained to search before committing. "Commercial fit-out contractor Sydney" is a higher-intent search than "blue denim jacket" — not a lower one. Google Ads captures the moment of maximum buying intent regardless of industry.

The economics work even better for high-value services

This is where service businesses have a structural advantage that almost nobody talks about. A retailer selling a $45 product needs to acquire a customer for under $8 to make the economics work. If your average job is $6,000, you can profitably pay $300 per lead — and still produce exceptional returns on a modest ad budget.

Trades — Electrical, Plumbing, HVAC, Refrigeration

Trades have a dual market: emergency/reactive work (leaking pipe, no power) and planned work (renovation, new build). Each requires a different campaign structure.

High-intent keyword patterns

emergency electrician [suburb]licensed plumber near mecommercial HVAC contractor [city]after hours electricianswitchboard upgrade costgas plumber [suburb]

What actually works

Split emergency and planned work into separate campaigns — different intent, different bid logic, different landing pages.
Call extensions and call-only ads outperform click-to-website for emergency searches — people need a phone number, not a website.
Location bid adjustments within 15–20km of your operating area typically lift conversion rate 25–40% vs broad geographic targeting.
Negative keywords like "DIY," "course," "how to," and "apprenticeship" filter out research traffic that will never convert.
🏗

Construction — Builders, Renovators, Commercial Fit-Out

Construction has the longest buying cycle of any trade category — often 3–6 months from first search to signed contract. This changes campaign structure fundamentally.

High-intent keyword patterns

custom home builder [suburb]kitchen renovation quotecommercial office fit-out [city]house extension builders near mewarehouse construction contractorknock down rebuild [suburb]

What actually works

Remarketing is essential — with a 3–6 month decision cycle, someone who clicked your ad in month one needs to keep seeing you in months two through five.
A lead magnet (like this guide) works exceptionally well for construction — buyers in the research phase will trade an email for a "renovation cost guide" before they're ready to call.
Ad copy that disqualifies ("for projects $50k+") improves lead quality and reduces wasted quote time — even if it lowers total click volume.
Google Local Service Ads (LSAs) run alongside Search Ads and show a "Google Guaranteed" badge — high credibility for residential construction.
📋

Professional Services — Accounting, Legal, Consulting, Engineering

Professional services buyers are typically more research-intensive, more sceptical of sales pressure, and more responsive to demonstrated expertise than to direct offers.

High-intent keyword patterns

accountant for small business [suburb]commercial lawyer near mebusiness consultant Melbournestructural engineer residentialtax accountant for tradesHR consultant [city]

What actually works

Landing pages for professional services need to establish credibility first — qualifications, years of experience, types of clients — before any CTA.
A "free initial consultation" offer outperforms generic CTAs because it frames the first contact as low-risk and expertise-based.
Audience targeting by in-market segment ("small business owners," "new home buyers") combined with keyword targeting lifts relevance and reduces wasted clicks.
Exact match and phrase match only — professional services searches are specific enough that broad match will attract irrelevant traffic at premium CPCs.
Chapter 3

The 4-stage campaign framework for service businesses

Service businesses require a different campaign architecture than retail. Here's the framework that works consistently across trades, construction and professional services.

STAGE 01
Target — Find buying intent, not browsing
Focus on exact and phrase match keywords that signal someone is ready to hire, not researching. Pair with tight geographic targeting — 15–25km radius from your service area. Build a negative keyword list of 100+ terms before day one.
"commercial electrician dandenong" — yes. "how to wire a switchboard" — excluded before launch.
STAGE 02
Qualify — Let the ad do the filtering
Ad copy for service businesses should include qualifying signals that attract serious buyers and filter out price-shoppers: project minimum ("from $5,000"), experience signals ("20+ years"), and specificity about what you do and who you serve.
"Commercial fitouts from $80k · Licensed builder · South Melbourne — Get a quote."
STAGE 03
Convert — Landing page built for phone calls
Don't send service traffic to your homepage. A dedicated landing page with one goal (phone call or form submission), trust signals above the fold, and a frictionless CTA consistently outperforms a homepage by 2–5×. No navigation menu. One action.
Phone number in the header. "Get a free quote" form. 3 trust signals. No other links.
STAGE 04
Measure — Cost per qualified lead, not clicks
Set up call tracking before day one. Track calls as conversions (not just form fills). Optimise toward cost-per-qualified-lead — not CPC, not CTR. A $14 CPC that produces a $180 CPL beats a $4 CPC with a $550 CPL every time.
Target metric: cost per quoted job, not cost per click.
Chapter 4

5 mistakes service businesses make with Google Ads

These five patterns appear in the vast majority of trade and service Google Ads accounts we've reviewed. Each one is fixable.

01

Running broad match keywords with no negative list

Broad match for "plumber" will show your ad for "plumber apprenticeship jobs," "how to become a plumber," and "plumber memes." A 200-term negative keyword list built before launch eliminates most of this waste.

→ Fix: Exact and phrase match only for the first 90 days. Build negatives from search term reports weekly.
02

Sending clicks to the homepage

A homepage has navigation, multiple messages, and asks the visitor to find their own path. For someone who just searched "emergency plumber," that friction costs conversions. A dedicated landing page removes the decision and increases conversion rate 2–5×.

→ Fix: One landing page per campaign. One goal per page. No menu navigation.
03

No call tracking — so ROI is a guess

Most service businesses win jobs over the phone, not through website forms. Without call tracking tied to Google Ads, there's no way to know which campaigns produce revenue — so budget decisions are made on gut feel, not data.

→ Fix: Set up call tracking (Google's native or a third-party tool) before spending a dollar. Track calls as conversions.
04

Judging performance after two weeks

Google's Smart Bidding algorithm needs a minimum of 30–50 conversions to exit the "learning phase" and optimise effectively. Pausing or restructuring campaigns in week two restarts the learning clock and produces permanently underperforming accounts.

→ Fix: Commit to a 90-day evaluation period. Optimise keywords and copy; don't restructure campaigns in the first month.
05

Optimising for clicks instead of qualified leads

A low CPC from broad traffic that never converts is expensive, not cheap. The only number that matters for service businesses is cost per qualified lead — and the only way to get there is to track, and optimise toward, actual enquiries.

→ Fix: Set conversion actions for calls and form fills. Use Target CPA bidding with a realistic CPL target based on your average job value.
