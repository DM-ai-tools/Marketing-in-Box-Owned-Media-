Pixel Foundation
Is your tracking code installed and firing correctly?
0/2 ▼
—
Your Meta Pixel fires on every page of your website — including product pages, cart, checkout, and confirmation.
A pixel that only fires on the homepage misses 80%+ of purchase-intent signals. Without full-funnel events, Meta's algorithm can't find buyers like your best customers.
Where to check: Meta Events Manager → Event Testing → browse your site and watch events fire in real time.
—
A Purchase event fires every time an order is confirmed — triggered by a purchase event, not just a URL visit to the thank-you page.
URL-based thank-you page triggers miss ~15–30% of purchases (cart abandonment recoveries, direct payments, alt checkouts). Undercounted purchases = underfunded campaigns.
Where to check: Events Manager → Purchase event → filter last 7 days → compare to actual Shopify/WooCommerce orders.

Conversions API (CAPI)
Is server-side tracking filling in what the browser misses?
0/2 ▼
—
You have Conversions API (CAPI) set up and running alongside your browser pixel.
iOS 14+ privacy changes blocked ~30–40% of browser pixel events. CAPI sends conversion data server-side, bypassing browser restrictions. Without CAPI, you're invisible to a third of your buyers.
Where to check: Events Manager → Data Sources → your pixel → Event Overview. Look for events showing both "Browser" and "Server" under Received.
—
In Events Manager, your server events show "Deduplicated" status — not "Redundant" or appearing as doubles with no deduplication key.
CAPI without proper deduplication doubles your reported purchases, making ROAS look artificially high. Inflated ROAS means misallocated budget — you'll scale the wrong campaigns.
Where to check: Events Manager → Purchase event → look for a "Deduplicated" badge. If you see "Redundant", deduplication isn't working.
🪟
Attribution Window
Are you counting the conversions that actually belong to your ads?
0/2 ▼
—
Your account attribution setting includes "1-day view" in addition to 7-day click (i.e., it is set to 7-day click + 1-day view, not 7-day click only).
View-through conversions — purchases after seeing but not clicking an ad — account for 15–30% of Meta-attributed revenue for most retail brands. Excluding them means under-crediting your best awareness campaigns.
Where to check: Ads Manager → Campaign level → Columns → Attribution Setting column. Or: Account Settings → Attribution.
—
You've checked your attribution window against your actual purchase cycle length — and they match (e.g., if customers typically buy within 3 days of clicking, you aren't using a 28-day window).
Mismatched attribution windows either under- or over-count. A 28-day window for an impulse-buy product inflates ROAS and causes you to overspend on underperforming campaigns.
Where to check: Ads Manager → Columns → Compare attribution windows. Compare 1-day click vs 7-day click vs 28-day click side by side.
🎯
Event Match Quality
Is Meta matching your conversions to real user profiles?
0/2 ▼
—
Your Event Match Quality (EMQ) score for Purchase events is 6.0 or higher in Events Manager.
EMQ measures how well Meta can match conversion events to actual people in its system. An EMQ below 6.0 means Meta can't attribute and optimise properly — your ROAS is structurally capped.
Where to check: Events Manager → your pixel → Event Match Quality column next to your Purchase event.
—
Advanced Matching is enabled — your pixel passes hashed customer data (email address, phone number) with each conversion event.
Advanced Matching typically lifts EMQ by 1.5–2 points, improving attribution by 20–30%. Without it, every conversion that happens in a logged-out browser or after cookies expire is invisible to Meta.
Where to check: Events Manager → Settings → Advanced Matching → toggle should be On. Verify parameters in Event Testing → "em" and "ph" fields present.
📶
Signal Volume & Optimisation
Is Meta's algorithm getting enough data to optimise effectively?
0/2 ▼
—
Your primary conversion campaign's ad sets each receive at least 50 purchase conversion events per week.
Meta's algorithm needs ~50 conversions per ad set per week to exit the "Learning Phase" and optimise efficiently. Below 50 events/week, the algorithm is effectively guessing — and charging you for the privilege.
Where to check: Ads Manager → Ad Set level → Delivery column. "Learning Phase" or "Learning Limited" means you're below the threshold.
—
You're optimising for the lowest-funnel conversion event that still generates 50+ events per week (e.g., Purchase rather than Add to Cart, if volumes allow).
Optimising for Add to Cart when you could use Purchase gives Meta a weaker signal. Add-to-Cart buyers and Purchase buyers don't look the same to the algorithm — optimising for the wrong one finds cheaper but less valuable traffic.
Where to check: Ads Manager → Ad Set level → Optimisation & Delivery → Conversion Event. If it says Add to Cart but you get 50+ purchases/week, switch.
