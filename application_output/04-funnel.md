# PART 1: FUNNEL STRATEGY & ARCHITECTURE

## 1A. Strategic Rationale

Rachel Nguyen does not search for "social media marketing agency." Per her ICP research-behaviour profile, she searches in scattered, problem-framed terms — "how to reduce agency staff turnover," "social media workflow tools for agencies," "why is client social engagement dropping" — and she is more likely to encounter a solution through **peer networks, LinkedIn groups for Australian agency leaders, and informal conversation** than through a direct commercial search. She is Problem-Aware, not Solution-Aware: she has logged the issue internally as "social is becoming a liability" but has not yet framed it as "we need a specialist SMM partner."

Two structural facts follow directly from that:

1. **A direct consultation ask is the wrong first move for her.** She isn't the final decision-maker, she hasn't built an internal business case yet, and jumping straight to "book a call" skips every step she's actually stuck on (Barriers and Uncertainties, ICP §8). What she needs first is validation that her situation is common and structural, not a personal failure of hers or her team's — which is precisely the register the ICP's own "Market Opportunities and Positioning" section (§13) recommends: *educational and validating content that names the symptoms she's already living, without yet pushing a solution category.*
2. **A community mechanic matches her actual channel behaviour** (peer conversation, LinkedIn, meetups) far better than a gated PDF or a hard CTA. It also gives TrafficRadius a standing, low-pressure presence in exactly the informal research loop she already uses — instead of hoping she stumbles on the pillar page through a solution-category search she isn't running yet.

Meanwhile, the pillar page already has a strong, proven, low-friction mechanic for the brand-side reader: the hero audit widget, independently flagged in the pillar's own CRO audit as *"the best asset on the page… self-selecting, low friction."* There's no reason to replace it — the job here is to **give it a standalone landing page** so it can be driven by dedicated traffic (paid social, retargeting, blog CTAs) without competing for space inside the pillar, and so its thank-you page can do more selling work than a modal ever could.

The **dual-path entry page** is the mechanism that reconciles both needs in one funnel: a single arrival page that reads the room in one line and lets the visitor self-select in under five seconds — "I run social for my own brand" → Free Audit; "I run social for other people's clients" → The Agency Delivery Desk. This mirrors exactly how the pillar itself signposts the agency lane ("Are you an agency, not a brand?") rather than contradicting it.

**Connection to the conversion goal.** The stated goal is cold → warm, not cold → booked. Both paths are therefore designed to capture a qualified, tagged lead and hand them to a nurture sequence before any hard ask — the audit path delivers a finding and *then* nurtures toward the call; the community path delivers belonging and proof-of-relevance and *then* nurtures toward the call. Both converge on the same consultation page, which is where the pillar's existing "Book your free 30-minute strategy call" promise (30 minutes, no obligation, written summary afterwards) is honoured exactly as already built.

**Connection to the pillar's CTA structure.** Nothing here invents a new CTA ladder — it fills in the ladder that already existed with a gap in it. The pillar names four rungs; rung 2 ("Download the agency delivery checklist") was explicitly flagged `[CLIENT TO CONFIRM: asset exists]`. This funnel makes that asset real by building it as the community's entry gift, so the pillar's own placeholder resolves the moment this funnel ships.
