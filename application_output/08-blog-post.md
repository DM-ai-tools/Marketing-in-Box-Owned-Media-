If you've searched for "social media marketing cost" more than once this month, you're not just curious — you're trying to work out whether the money already going out the door is doing anything. Maybe you're paying a freelancer $800 a month and can't say what it's returned. Maybe an agency quoted you $4,500 and you have no idea if that's reasonable. Or maybe you're running it in-house and the real cost — the hours, the turnover, the campaigns nobody's tracking properly — never shows up on an invoice at all.

This article gives you real Australian pricing bands for every delivery model, the specific factors that move the number up or down, and — more importantly — why the businesses spending the most on social media marketing aren't always the ones getting the most qualified leads out of it. Cost and conversion are two different problems, and most guides only answer one of them.

## What Does Social Media Marketing Actually Cost in Australia?

There's no single number, because "social media marketing" isn't one service — it's four genuinely different delivery models, and each one has its own cost structure, its own hidden costs, and its own failure mode. Here's how they actually compare.

### DIY / Self-Managed

Direct cash cost: near zero beyond scheduling software (typically $20–$120/month) and any ad spend you choose to run. The real cost is time — realistically 5–10 hours a week for a business posting across two to three platforms with any consistency, more if you're also handling design and community replies. Most businesses underestimate this until the person doing it goes on leave and the account visibly stalls. Our [organic social media management](/organic-social-media-management/) page breaks down what "doing it properly" actually involves if you're weighing this option seriously.

### Freelancer

Typically $500–$2,500/month in Australia depending on scope (content only vs. content + paid + reporting) and experience level. Cheapest entry point into professional-looking output, but you're buying one skill set — usually strong on content, weaker on paid campaign structure, tracking and strategy. Continuity risk is real: if they get sick, take on other clients, or move on, there's no backup.

### In-House Hire

A junior-to-mid social media coordinator in Australia typically costs $60,000–$85,000 in salary, plus roughly 20–30% in on-costs (super, leave, software licences, recruitment), before you've factored in management time or leave cover. That's a meaningful annual commitment for one skill set, and it resets to zero the moment that person resigns — which, across the industry, happens more often in junior social roles than in almost any other marketing function.

### Agency / Specialist Plan

Across the Australian market, ongoing social media marketing retainers generally sit in three bands: a lower band for single-platform, content-only support; a mid band for multi-platform management with paid campaigns included; and a higher band for full-funnel management across several platforms with dedicated strategy and reporting. Exact figures vary by scope, platform count, and content volume — [book a free consultation](#final-cta) and we'll walk you through where your specific requirements land, with a written figure and no obligation attached.

## What Actually Drives the Price Up or Down

"It depends" is technically true and completely unhelpful. Here's what it actually depends on:

- **Number of platforms.** Running two platforms properly costs less — and converts better — than running five thinly. This is the single biggest lever most businesses get wrong; they spread the same budget across more channels instead of concentrating it.
- **Content volume.** The jump from 8 posts a month to daily posting plus Stories and Reels roughly doubles production time, and production time is most of what you're paying for.
- **Paid campaign complexity.** A single boosted-post campaign costs less to manage than a structured funnel with cold, warm and retargeting audiences — but the funnel is what actually produces trackable leads.
- **Community management load.** Comment and DM volume on a growing account can quietly become a part-time job on its own.
- **Reporting depth.** A basic analytics screenshot costs nothing extra. A monthly report that ties spend to actual enquiries and sales requires tracking infrastructure most freelancers and junior in-house staff have never been asked to set up.

Notice what's missing from that list: the platform itself isn't the main cost driver. Two businesses both running "Instagram and Facebook" can have management fees three times apart, because one is posting content with no tracking and the other is running a structured, measured funnel.

## The Real Problem Isn't the Price — It's the Conversion Gap

Here's the uncomfortable pattern we see constantly: a business increases its social media budget, the posting gets more consistent, engagement even ticks up — and enquiries stay flat. The spend goes up. The result doesn't. Whoever owns that decision internally is now the one who has to explain a rising line item with no clear return, and "the content looks better" doesn't hold up in a budget review.

This is the actual question hiding behind "what does social media marketing cost" — not "is this number fair," but "if I spend this, will I actually get more qualified leads out of it, or just more likes." Cost is easy to quote. Conversion is the part that separates a genuinely useful plan from an expensive content calendar.

## Why More Spend Doesn't Automatically Mean More Leads

Three things break the link between social media spend and actual leads, and none of them are about how much you're paying.

**No tracking infrastructure.** Without Meta Pixel, conversion API, and properly configured Google Analytics 4 event tracking, you can report reach and engagement — but you cannot say which post, campaign or ad actually produced an enquiry. Most freelancer and in-house setups skip this because it's technical, unglamorous, and doesn't show up in a content calendar.

**No retargeting layer.** Cold-audience posts and ads reach people who've never heard of you; only a fraction convert on the first touch. Without a retargeting funnel aimed at people who've already visited your site or engaged with your content, you're paying repeatedly to reach cold strangers instead of following up with warm prospects who are considerably more likely to convert.

**Vanity metrics standing in for business metrics.** Follower growth and engagement rate feel like progress, but neither one pays your invoices. A social media plan optimised toward reach will produce reach. A plan optimised toward enquiries, bookings and sales requires different targeting, different creative, and different reporting from day one — and it's rarely the default setup.

## In-House vs. Freelancer vs. Agency: Which Actually Converts Better?

Cost comparisons usually stop at the invoice. Here's the comparison that actually matters — which model is built to convert, not just to publish.

| | **DIY / In-house** | **Freelancer** | **Specialist Plan** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Content consistency** | Strong until the person is busy or leaves | Depends entirely on their availability | Backed by a system, not one person |
| **Paid campaign structure** | Rarely goes beyond boosted posts | Varies widely by individual skill | Structured cold/warm/retargeting funnels |
| **Tracking and attribution** | Usually basic or absent | Usually basic | Pixel, conversion API, GA4 as standard |
| **What gets optimised toward** | Whatever's easiest to measure | Whatever the brief specifies | Enquiries, bookings and sales |
| **Continuity risk** | High — resets on resignation | Moderate — one point of failure | Low — team-based delivery |
| **Reporting you can defend upward** | Rare | Occasional | Standard monthly deliverable |

The pattern worth noticing: cost and conversion capability don't move together in a straight line. A $1,200/month freelancer with no tracking can cost you more in wasted ad spend than a properly structured $3,500/month plan that's actually converting — because the freelancer's number only measures production, not return.

## How to Get More Qualified Leads From the Budget You Already Have

Before increasing spend anywhere, these are the fixes that typically move the needle first — regardless of which delivery model you're using:

**Narrow your platforms deliberately.** If your budget is spread across five platforms, concentrate it on the two where your actual buyers spend attention. For B2B audiences, that's usually [LinkedIn](/linkedin-ads/) first; for visually-driven retail, homewares or lifestyle brands, [Pinterest](/pinterest-ads/) consistently outperforms expectations; for reaching younger or trend-engaged audiences, [TikTok](/tiktok-ads/) is often underused relative to its return.

**Install tracking before you scale spend.** Meta Pixel and GA4 conversion tracking should be running before you increase your [Meta Ads](/meta-ads/) budget, not after. Scaling an untracked campaign just scales the uncertainty.

**Add a retargeting layer.** If you're only running cold-audience campaigns, you're leaving your most convertible audience — people who already know you — untouched. This is usually the single fastest efficiency gain available.

**Separate organic credibility from paid volume.** Paid ads sending traffic to an inconsistent or outdated profile convert worse than the same ads sending traffic to an active, current one. If your organic presence has gone quiet, that's worth fixing before you spend more on ads pointing at it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does social media marketing cost in Australia?**
It depends on the delivery model. DIY costs mostly time (5–10+ hours weekly). Freelancers typically run $500–$2,500/month. An in-house junior coordinator typically costs $60,000–$85,000 annually plus on-costs. Agency retainers vary by platform count, content volume and paid campaign complexity — you'll get a written, scoped figure after a consultation, with no obligation to proceed.

**Is it more cost-effective to hire in-house or work with an agency?**
Hiring in-house buys one skill set and carries continuity risk — output stops if that person leaves or is on leave. A plan typically gives you a strategist, designer, copywriter and paid media manager for a comparable or lower monthly cost than a single salaried hire once on-costs, software and recruitment are factored in, and it doesn't resign.

**Why isn't my social media spend generating leads?**
Usually one of three things: no conversion tracking installed (so you can't see what's working), no retargeting layer (so you're only reaching cold audiences once), or a plan optimised toward reach and engagement rather than enquiries. Higher spend without fixing these first tends to produce more of the same result, just at a higher cost.

**How long does it take to see results from social media marketing?**
Paid campaigns can generate clicks and enquiries within days of launch, though early weeks are as much about gathering data as delivering volume. Organic growth typically takes a few months to build real traction. Results vary by industry, starting position and budget — anyone promising a fixed timeline without knowing your starting point is guessing.

**What's included in a typical social media marketing plan?**
Strategy, a documented content calendar, copywriting, design and short-form video production, scheduling, community management, conversion tracking, and a monthly performance report. Ad spend itself, influencer fees, and full video production shoots are usually scoped separately. Full detail is on our [social media marketing services](/social-media-marketing/) page.

**Do you offer support for agencies managing social media for their own clients?**
Some of our work sits behind other agencies as overflow capacity or specialist delivery — different scope and commercial structure to a direct brand engagement. If that's your situation, our [B2B social media marketing](/b2b-social-media-marketing/) page is the better starting point, or raise it directly on a consultation call.

## The Bottom Line

The number on a quote or a payroll line tells you almost nothing on its own — what matters is whether that spend is structured to produce trackable enquiries, or just consistent posting. Before comparing another price, get clear on whether tracking, retargeting and platform focus are actually in place; fixing those often produces a bigger lift in qualified leads than any amount of extra budget.
