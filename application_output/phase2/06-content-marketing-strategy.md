### 2.2 What Each Competitor Does Exceptionally Well

**Zib Digital**
- Runs content strategy as an explicit "pipeline-tied roadmap," not a standalone calendar — every piece is positioned to feed a named downstream channel.
- Meta Business Partner status is used as a visible trust signal directly on the paid social page.
- Quantifies outcomes at the top-line level (100M+ impressions, 20x ROAS) rather than burying results in prose.

**Digital Deluxe**
- Every content piece ships with a stated distribution plan (email, social, paid amplification) attached at the brief stage, not bolted on after publishing.
- Reports content performance against pipeline/revenue rather than traffic — a genuinely mature attribution posture.

**The Meaningful Social Club**
- Explicitly sequences its funnel in copy: "with your content ready, we build and manage your ad campaigns" — a structural narrative competitors rarely state this plainly.
- Attributes paid performance partly to organic creative quality in its named case study, tying two service lines together credibly.

**Impressive Digital**
- Broad service integration (SEO/SEM + Meta Ads + content) suggests genuine full-funnel capability, though the pipeline isn't demonstrated on a single page — an execution gap, not a strategy gap.

**Dreamworx Marketing**
- States plainly that "the most successful businesses combine paid advertising with a strong content strategy" — a clear, quotable thesis statement other competitors leave implicit.

### 2.3 Identified Content Gaps (Client Opportunities)

1. **No competitor publishes transparent Meta Ads cost/pricing content.** None of the five discloses pricing structure, fee bands, or a cost-benchmarking tool. TrafficRadius already has this built (Pricing Guide) — this content programme's job is to surround it with organic search demand. *Acted on in Cluster C, Section 5 (existing-asset fold-in), Section 4.1 keyword P0.*
2. **No competitor targets the agency-operations / white-label overflow buyer.** All five sell content-plus-Meta bundles direct to brands. None write for a Head of Client Services managing Meta Ads across a client portfolio. This is Rachel's exact position and it is entirely unclaimed. *Acted on in Cluster A and the Primary Persona weighting of the editorial calendar.*
3. **None of the five content programmes address the specific, scattered, problem-aware search language this ICP actually uses** ("why is client social engagement dropping," "how to reduce agency staff turnover") — competitors write category-aware content ("Meta Ads services"), not problem-aware content. *Acted on directly in Cluster A subtopic naming and Section 4.3 long-tail keywords.*
4. **Attribution honesty is absent industry-wide.** Every competitor's case studies present ROAS/impression figures without disclosing iOS/cross-device/view-through limitations. TrafficRadius's own brand voice already commits to this honesty — no competitor is building trust on it publicly. *Acted on in Cluster C ("Why You Can't 'Prove Exactly' What Meta Ads Returned").*
5. **No competitor publishes vertical-specific Meta Ads playbooks** matching TrafficRadius's own named industries (trades, hospitality, fitness, retail, professional services, education, construction). Content is generic across verticals for all five. *Acted on in Cluster D.*

## 3. Target Audience & Buyer Personas

*Every content brief in this plan must declare which of the three personas below it is written for — no brief may target "general readers."*

### Primary — Rachel Nguyen, "The Overstretched Agency Ops Lead"
- **Role:** Head of Client Services, ~95-staff Melbourne full-service digital agency
- **Geography:** Melbourne HQ, agency services clients Australia-wide
- **Current state:** Problem-aware, not solution-aware; hasn't yet framed her problem as "we need a Meta Ads/social partner"
- **Content consumption:** Scattered, operational search terms ("how to reduce agency staff turnover"); LinkedIn peer groups and agency-leader communities; occasional downloadable guides framed around agency delivery pain, not generic social media tips; passive discovery more than active category research
- **Core pain (her words):** "Every time someone leaves, we're back to square one." / "We don't really know what this should cost." / "I'm worried one of our bigger accounts is going to churn over this."
- **Decision trigger:** A client formally raises social performance in a retainer review, or a second team resignation makes the capacity gap undeniable
- **Preferred format:** Short, specific guides and checklists she can screenshot or forward internally without booking a call first

### Secondary — "The Agency Director," the actual economic decision-maker
- **Role:** Co-founder/Director at the same or comparable agency, one level above Rachel; final sign-off on any external spend
- **Geography:** Same Melbourne/national agency footprint
- **Current state:** Rarely browses content directly — reached almost exclusively via what Rachel forwards or summarises upward
- **Content consumption:** Skims one-pagers, PDF summaries, and business-case documents; time-poor; wants risk and cost framed plainly
- **Core pain:** Protecting client retention and margin without approving spend he/she can't defend to the board or to their own P&L
- **Decision trigger:** A credible, quantified business case with a stated cost range and comparison against the cost of hiring internally
- **Preferred format:** Executive-summary-style content — comparison tables, cost breakdowns, and named risk mitigations, not long-form narrative blogs

### Tertiary — "The Switching Marketing Manager," a competitor-frustrated brand-side buyer
- **Role:** In-house marketing manager at a mid-market brand (retail, hospitality, trades, fitness — per TrafficRadius's own named verticals), currently running Meta Ads through an existing agency, freelancer, or in-house effort
- **Geography:** Melbourne and interstate, national delivery acceptable
- **Current state:** Solution-aware and actively re-evaluating; frustrated with current reporting, cost, or creative fatigue
- **Content consumption:** Comparison-oriented searches ("meta ads agency vs in-house," "how much should meta ads cost"), reads case studies and comparison tables closely, checks Google reviews and comparison pages before booking a call
- **Core pain:** "I can't tell if what I'm paying is fair" and "I can't prove what this is actually returning"
- **Decision trigger:** A specific, named red flag in their current arrangement (cost opacity, stagnant results, poor reporting) surfaces during a budget review
- **Preferred format:** Decision-stage comparison content, vertical-specific case studies, and interactive tools (matches the existing Health Check quiz well)

## 8. Distribution & Amplification Plan

*Only channels confirmed available (GBP, email) are planned below. LinkedIn/social amplification, paid amplification, and PR/backlink outreach are omitted — none were confirmed as available channels in the inputs. If any become available, Sections 7.3–7.5 of the original framework should be reactivated at that point.*

### 8.1 Organic Distribution (Always-On)

- **On-page SEO checklist per article:** target keyword in title tag (50–60 characters) and H1, meta description 150–155 characters including the primary keyword and a specific benefit, one featured-snippet-formatted FAQ block per piece.
- **Internal linking minimum:** 1 link up to `/meta-ads/`, 1 link up to `/social-media-marketing/` where topically relevant, 2–3 sideways links to sibling cluster articles, 1 link to the relevant existing lead magnet (Pricing Guide or Health Check) where the topic warrants it.
- **Schema markup:** `Article` on every long-form piece; `FAQPage` where an FAQ block is present; `HowTo` on the execution-focused Cluster B pieces (e.g. retargeting funnel structure, creative testing framework).
- **Search Console monitoring cadence:** weekly, folded into the writer's publishing routine (Section 9) — checking index status and early query data on each new piece before the following week's publish.

### 8.2 Google Business Profile (Local Distribution)

- **Cadence:** 2 GBP "Update" posts per week, repurposed from that week's long-form article (per Section 7).
- **Structure:** short benefit-led copy (100–150 words), one image or simple graphic, CTA to the relevant lead magnet or `/meta-ads-strategy-call/`.
- **Local keyword variants:** each post should carry a Melbourne or Victoria-specific phrase where genuinely relevant (avoid forcing "Melbourne" into every post — only where the underlying article is locally framed, e.g. the trades or hospitality vertical pieces).
- **Review-response cadence:** ongoing, as reviews arrive — not tied to the content calendar but tracked in the same weekly routine.

### 8.3 Email Distribution

- **Newsletter cadence:** monthly (aligned to Weeks 4, 8, 12), structured as: one cluster-article spotlight, 2–3 supporting headlines from that period, one CTA to the relevant lead magnet or consultation booking.
- **Segmentation logic:** reuse the existing Pricing Guide funnel's segmentation fields (role: agency ops vs. brand-side marketing manager) to send Cluster A/agency-ops content to Rachel-type subscribers and Cluster D/vertical content to brand-side subscribers, with Cluster C (cost/measurement) content sent to both segments.
- **Nurture sequence integration:** the existing 3-email Pricing Guide sequence (Day 0/2/5) remains the seed sequence for new downloads; new cluster content is used to extend nurture for subscribers who complete that sequence without booking, giving a second and third touch point drawn from Clusters A and C specifically (the two clusters most directly tied to her stated objections).

### 10.3 Quality Checklist (Pre-Publish)

- **Originality/AI standard:** no unedited AI output published; every draft is fact-checked and rewritten in TrafficRadius's established voice before publish.
- **Accuracy/sourcing standard (ACCC / Australian Consumer Law-aligned):** no invented statistics; every numeric claim sourced or explicitly labelled an estimate; no "guaranteed" outcome language; any testimonial or case-study figure carries a "results vary" qualifier and is only published with confirmed consent and a genuine basis, consistent with the substantiation and misleading-conduct obligations under the Australian Consumer Law that the ACCC enforces.
- **Voice/localisation standard:** Australian English, Melbourne-first geo framing where relevant; locked terminology applied (**plan**, **consultation/free strategy call**, **agency**, **more qualified leads**, **less wasted ad spend**); banned terms (**cheap**, **guaranteed rankings**, **growth hacking**, **risk free**, and near-variants) checked and absent.
- **Compliance standard:** reviewed against ACCC/Australian Consumer Law guidance on testimonials, comparative advertising, and substantiation of performance claims before publish.
- **Accessibility standard:** WCAG 2.1 AA — descriptive alt text on all images, single H1 per page, logical H2/H3 hierarchy, descriptive (non-"click here") link text.

- **Weekly:** publish + leading-indicator check + interlink audit (folded into the writer's existing routine — no additional headcount required).
